# Agent briefs: verify (stage 4), each in a fresh agent

Prefix each with `briefs-common.md`. The input file holds the row's research.

**Second pass on a Yes** (each Oracle product, the Edge AI verdict, the offer choice)
> Try to disprove this claim; confirm it only if the evidence survives. Claim: <…>. Cited source: <…>.
> Search on your own first, then test for:
> - a job-ad line that names it only as a skill;
> - a copy you cannot trace to the company;
> - an undated page;
> - evidence older than 18 months;
> - a newer migration.
>
> For an internal source, quote `decks.txt` exactly. For the offer choice, also test the rejected offer: is there really no team it would serve?
> Verdict: confirmed, narrowed or failed, with evidence items (url, date, quote, check) and each test's result.

**Signals**
> Open every signal in `<input.json>`. Confirm the quote, the true date (for a job ad, open or closed today) and the team it comes from.
> A careers page that redirects to its homepage proves nothing about an ad; date a closure from a dated copy.
> Regrade with the rubric. Recommend keep, regrade or drop, with a reason and corrected text. A closed ad is "sought".
> A Sales Navigator item stays unopened: confirm it on the public web and grade it on its text.

**Facts and the hook**
> For each fact the two-pager will print (identity, HQ, sizes, programs, units, people in `<input.json>`), give a verdict (correct, wrong, outdated or unsupported), a dated source and corrected wording.
> Find what the likely problem costs the business in money, with the arithmetic marked "(inferred)".
> Confirm each champion's title from a public source, or say there is none.
> Name the strongest current public sign that AI is on the radar of <the team the offer serves> (a hire, mandate, program or budget) and the strongest pain trigger in the exact work, or say plainly that none exists.
