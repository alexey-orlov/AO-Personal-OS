# Agent briefs: verify (stage 4), each in a fresh agent

Prefix each with `briefs-common.md`. The input file holds the row's research.

**Second pass on a Yes** (each Oracle product, the Edge AI verdict, the offer choice)
> Independent second pass on one claim. Try to disprove it; confirm it only if the evidence survives.
> Claim: <…>. Cited source: <…>.
> Search on your own first. Then test for:
> - a job-ad line that names it only as a skill;
> - a copy you cannot trace to the company;
> - an undated page;
> - evidence older than 18 months;
> - a newer migration.
>
> For an internal source, read `decks.txt` and report its exact wording.
> For the offer choice, also test the rejected offer: is there really no team it would serve?
> Verdict: confirmed, narrowed or failed, with evidence items (url, date, quote, check) and each test's result.

**Signals**
> Open every signal in `<input.json>`. Confirm:
> - the quote;
> - the true date, and for a job ad, whether it is open or closed today;
> - the team it comes from.
>
> Regrade with the rubric. Recommend keep, regrade or drop, with a reason, and give corrected text.
> A closed ad is "sought". A Sales Navigator item stays unopened: search the public web for confirmation and grade it on its text.

**Facts and the hook**
> For each fact the two-pager will print (identity, HQ, sizes, programs, units, people in `<input.json>`), give a verdict (correct, wrong, outdated or unsupported), a dated source and corrected wording.
> Find what the likely problem costs the business in money, with the arithmetic marked "(inferred)".
> Confirm each champion's title from a public source, or say there is none.
> Name two hooks:
> - the strongest current public sign that AI is on the radar of <the team the offer serves> (a hire, mandate, program or budget);
> - the strongest pain trigger in the exact work.
>
> Or say plainly that none exists.
