## Key points
- **Stage visuals (slides 2–3 and the yellow stage cards):** more memorable visualisation through colour coding and one uniform structure and placement.
- **Data validation (slide 6):** the fields shown are examples, not the full list; add a second slide with the Product Types screenshot, where the business rules for allowed values per product type live.
- **Components info (slide 7):** generalise the criteria and list them as bullets instead of a comma-separated run.
- **Criteria slides (8 onward):** today's "required" conditions are guessed; CRM business-process screenshots will show each step's conditions — translate them into business meaning, still concrete. Slide 8 gets the Vendors screenshot, and its result must say the contract is signed, attached in the CRM, with its validity period or an auto-renewal note.
- **New scheme before slide 9:** Operations kick-off first, then contract process approval and delivery process approval in parallel. The kick-off is a joint meeting of everyone in servicing, contracting and the product, to discuss the product and process details and answer all questions; Operations then documents the processes with the teams and runs their approval. The step's meaning replaces the CRM card text.
- **Slides 10–11:** the owner of contract and delivery process approval is Operations, role **Business Process Lead**; the card is a hybrid of a to-do step and an approval step (document, discuss and describe the process with every department, record it in the CRM, approve). Contract process = signing and servicing the contract: billing, settlements with the client (and subcontractors where special), extra documents, accounting specifics. Delivery process = providing the product: access, issue or activation of an infrastructure service or the start of a service, through upgrades to deactivation or end of service.
- **Infrastructure step:** the meaning is building the infrastructure for public-infrastructure products up front, before customers can start using them.
- **Slide 12:** the Head of Product may move a step to after publication; name the reason, e.g. starting sales before the initial infrastructure investment, to build a first funnel when the investment's payback is uncertain — written concisely.
- **Slide 13 (Proof of concept):** R&D tests, deploys and uses the solution in near-client conditions, usually on R&D test stands; it is testing, distinct from the initial research that can be enough for a top-level architecture.
- **Wording:** "докази" as the translation of "evidence" reads too literally in Ukrainian; find a better word (e.g. "підтвердження").
- **Approvals order (slides 15–18):** Responsible for design → Head of product → Delivery → Finance. Explain the investments: the CRM's Vendor Commitments and Investment block records either a vendor commitment (e.g. prepayment or a yearly volume commitment regardless of customers) or an upfront infrastructure build (e.g. a public cluster built ahead of demand), with a USD amount; when amounts are set, Finance must approve.
- **Slide 16:** reuse slide 17's screenshot with the text changed to "Responsible for product delivery — approve product revision"; drop the "what do I approve" screenshot. After it, a table of product type → possible Responsible for delivery; the same for Responsible for design, with merged cells and a third column for scope. Delivery scope: provide the product end to end so the customer can start using it. Design scope splits in two: technical products (core and specialised infrastructure, add-ons) — R&D or another technical team (sometimes InfoSec) confirms the description matches the technical design, technical characteristics and limits are correct, and the component set and its mandatory parts are right; licences and services — the vendor or partner manager confirms the terms match contractual obligations with the vendor and that a contract exists.
- **Slide 20:** sending to billing happens automatically on publication or manually by the product manager when the automatic send failed (technical faults, CRM–billing desync).
- **Slides 21–22:** results — sales enablement held, with the recording and deck attached in the CRM checklist item and stored on SharePoint; for technical enablement, "received basic information and a product presentation, with a Q&A session", materials recorded.
- **Marketing brief:** explain it — when a product gets dedicated marketing promotion, marketing may request positioning information through its standard brief template; the product team fills it in and records the brief in the CRM.
- **Slides 24–25:** the scheme should run Operations kick-off → contract and delivery process approval in parallel → product approved and published → contract and delivery process confirmation in parallel, five elements, each step's meaning explained: every participant confirms this specific product fits the process, since a correctly described process can still not fit a particular product.
