## Product Experience

### 1) Please highlight what product management means at your current company (and prior) and what functions are/were you responsible for?

It was different at different roles and orgs I've been with:

- **Creatio (CRM products, marketing automation platform):** classical product management org, I owned:
  - *product strategy* (market analysis & customer insights, product vision & strategy, product roadmap);
  - *product discovery* (customer feedback management / user & customer research / product analytics);
  - *product design* (PRDs / UX & architecture design); this is owned by product function, done in collaboration with design and engineering;
  - *product delivery* ownership shared between Engineering & Product functions.
  - *product marketing* co-owned by marketing and product functions (product owns pricing & positioning; marketing owns packaging and product GTM activities)
- **Creatio (Product ecosystem, enabling products)** - I led cross-functional innovation product teams, owning:
  - *product* (the platform: strategy, discovery/design/delivery; partner products: supervision, advisory and certification);
  - *GTM* (sales enablement, business development & partnerships, marketing);
  - *engineering & support*;
  - *content* (documentation, e-learning, self-service SDKs - as a part of my responsibility for the Enterprise knowledge hub).
- **Jooble (Product innovation)**: product leaders of the new ventures we were testing and launching were more of a GMs, owning all aspects of a new product: strategy, product design and delivery, GTM & finance.

### 2) Where have you driven innovation to build something truly new and differentiated? What was the customer impact and commercial impact?

1. **Marketing Automation & lead management platform**
   - I built the company's marketing automation and lead management platform from the ground up. We launched it as an MVP, refined the product-market fit across segments, and then scaled the product.
   - *Novelty and differentiation:* within 2 years, the product was first included in Gartner's Magic Quadrant and then remained there for several consecutive years, advancing from a challenger to a leader in the CRM lead management and B2B marketing automation space. Its industry-leading lead-to-revenue analytics and marketing attribution capabilities, flexible campaign engine, and seamless integration between sales and marketing channels were key features that set the platform apart.
   - *Customer Impact:* In three years, the platform grew from 0 to over 400 enterprise customers who use it to automate omnichannel marketing campaigns and lead management.
   - *Commercial Impact:* launching this product helped the company expand from pure SFA into the marketing automation market and defend its positioning. The product ultimately began contributing up to 15% of the company's revenue.

2. **Product ecosystem & 3rd-party developer platform**
   - I launched and grew a platform for third-party developers to build and market their apps powered by Creatio's no-code platform.
   - *Novelty and differentiation:* the ecosystem uniquely focused on providing a framework not only for extensions and integrations but also for building end-to-end vertical or horizontal solutions on Creatio's platform. This enabled developers to create their own product businesses powered by the platform, clearly differentiating Creatio in the workflow automation space. To support this vision, we built a composable architecture and a best-in-class CI/CD framework, allowing seamless monthly releases of over 400 apps with very low adoption friction in highly customized enterprise environments, achieving best-in-class compatibility benchmarks.
   - *Customer Impact:* for partners, over 200 developers built their apps, including more than 50 standalone commercial software solutions powered by the platform, launching new third-party product businesses. For customers, within two years, more than 60% of the company's customers actively used at least one app for their automation needs, improving time-to-value and increasing ROI with the platform.
   - *Commercial Impact:* the growth of the product ecosystem enabled the company to transition its positioning from a configurable CRM to a no-code platform. The ecosystem became a new line of business and an additional revenue stream, contributing up to 4% of the company's revenue in two years.

3. **No-code ML engine for CRM applications**
   - Launched an ML predictive engine designed for sales, marketing, and service automation within Creatio's CRM portfolio.
   - *Novelty and differentiation:* starting with CRM use cases, we developed an ML engine that delivered on a no-code platform vision, allowing citizen developers to customize AI/ML functionalities without programming skills. The engine was designed to address real-world customer problems by implementing ML models that handle various business tasks, including classification (e.g., automatically routing support cases), predictive scoring and forecasting (e.g., lead scoring, opportunity forecasting, budget assessment), and recommendations (e.g., suggesting cross-sell products).
   - *Customer impact:* the ML features achieved adoption rates between 10% and 30%, which is notably high in the industry. The success stories I recall include a tech company reducing first-line support costs by approximately 30% and a retail bank implementing real-time product recommendations for account managers, boosting cross-sales revenue by up to 10%.
   - *Commercial impact:* the introduction of the ML engine enhanced the CRM's value proposition and provided a critical capability that began to emerge as a market must-have. The availability of these tools was a dealmaker in some enterprise deals.

### 3) How have you built a better understanding of customer requirements in your current role?

1. **Engage with live customers**
   - **Jooble - existing customers**: upon joining Jooble, I aimed to identify additional needs of the company's existing business customers and end users. My team and I conducted around 70 in-person interviews with businesses and end users, to draft initial conclusions about unserved needs of existing audiences.
   - **Jooble - new segments**: to explore new customer segments and their needs, we worked with the relevant industry experts to define different customer profiles and their needs, then recruited potential users through interview platforms and partner networks, conducting thorough interviews to gather insights from prospective customers in these new segments.
   - **Creatio - continuous discovery**: we established an ongoing process where each product leader (myself included) regularly talks to customers and end users, either focused on specific areas or to gather general customer insights, ensuring continuous alignment with customer needs.

2. **Aggregate and analyze customer feedback**
   - I led the establishment of a comprehensive feedback management system within Creatio's CRM products portfolio, aggregating data from support, sales, presales, implementation teams, and online user community. By building processes and tools to cluster and classify thousands of data points, we were able to really level up our understanding of customer problems, requests, root causes of their issues and pain points + got needed data and clarity to make conscious prioritization decisions.

3. **Participate in core customer-facing processes**
   - **Customer support**: when focusing on improving user satisfaction and customer retention, my PM team collaborated deeply with the customer support team, incl. working alongside support agents for several days. This hands-on experience was essential for empathizing with customers and understanding their issues deeply, beyond just reviewing support logs.
   - **Customer implementation**: when focusing on reducing the time to value for our customers, I and my team were deeply involved in several customer implementation projects. This participation provided direct insights into the challenges customers face during product setup and configuration.
   - **Pre-sales activities**: at Creatio, when launching a new product for the company's portfolio, I actively participated in sales demos and pre-sales activities. This involvement was crucial for calibrating our product-market fit for both existing and new customer segments.

### 4) What are the hallmarks of your product management approach?

1. **Ambitious and clear vision required.** I firmly believe that building something truly great requires an ambitious, differentiated, customer-focused vision - defining how the customer's life will improve in a meaningful and specific way through the product. A clear vision serves as your north star, guiding you to make excellent product decisions throughout the process. An ambitious vision drives an innovation mindset when you design your product features day-to-day.
2. **Quality customer insights.** Quality customer insights are at the core of good product decisions. Understanding what customers value, feel, aim for, and expect is essential for successful decision-making. While data and metrics help evaluate, ideate, and validate these insights, the foundation lies in building empathy and a true understanding of the customer. It's important to engage directly with live customers rather than relying solely on support tickets, sales requests, and product analytics.
3. **Customer is the priority #1.** The customer is the top priority. As a product leader and team, the primary focus is on increasing the value realized by the customer and enhancing their satisfaction and happiness with the product. This may involve short-term trade-offs and additional resource investments, which ultimately pay off in the long run.
4. **Details matter.** In successful products, edge cases, technical approaches, UX/UI, and product language are all important. While this might seem more trivial for consumer products, it is equally crucial for creating outstanding business products.

## Scale & Leadership Experience

### 1) When have you worked in an environment that was rapidly growing and scaling?

No doubt, my time at Creatio was definitely in a rapidly growing environment. During my 9 years there, Creatio grew from $10 million to $70 million in revenue and expanded from 280 to 700+ employees. The team often joked that "a year at Creatio is like three years elsewhere," referring to the fast pace, constant changes, and high intensity. The company consistently grew each year, including 30-40% growth in the last years of my tenure. The distinct features of that growth, which I experienced first-hand, included constant change (i.e. evolving processes and an organizational structure to support innovation goals and adapt to the dynamic changes), continuous bar raising, while maintaining a focus on efficiency (the mindset of "doing more with less" was in the company's DNA).

### 2) What in your view are the common challenges in a growth focused environment? How do you overcome these to capitalise on market opportunities?

Key challenges I have personally dealt with:

- **growing leaders & managers**: growth brings extended responsibilities and increased pressure on leaders. They often find themselves placed in between grown demands (due to business growth or new ambitious goals) and unscaled processes and teams that may lack necessary new skills / performance levels.
- **rapidly expanding teams**: expanding a team by more than 25-30% per year presents significant org challenges. Processes might not be ready to support such growth, and there may be insufficient capacity to properly onboard and integrate new team members. Also, if you don't lower the bar and continue prioritizing hard skills, soft skills, and cultural fit, it's obviously a recruiting challenge.
- **scaling operations**: unautomated processes, legacy technology, resistance to change, and operations that can't keep up with increased load.
- **keeping up with quality, debt & commitments**: as customer volumes and team sizes grow, gaps in quality assurance and tech debt become more evident and critical. Commitments made to specific customers pile up, overwhelming your product development.

Things to focus on to deal with these challenges (no step-by-step instruction exists - they are inherently tough):

- **A-player leaders**: make sure that all leadership and management roles are filled by A-players with a strong inherent growth mindset. If current leaders do not meet these requirements, replacing them should be a top priority.
- **Awareness & deliberate planning**: recognize growth challenges early and plan for them deliberately - is more efficient than firefighting (i.e., revise, improve, and structure bottleneck processes in advance, allocate more time for mentoring and coaching by senior employees and managers).
- **Ownership of the hiring process**: to successfully expand your team, build a highly efficient collaboration with your recruiting team and take ownership of the hiring process as a hiring manager.
- **Continuous improvement cadence**: establish a continuous improvement cadence for internal processes; this brings a more structured approach and makes it easier to deal with operational challenges.
- **Internal policies for custom requests**: develop organization-wide consensus on which customer requests to decline (based on the nature of request, customer segment, etc.).

### 5) Examples collaborating with CTO function and how you ensure that everyone is driving towards the same goals / business objectives?

Typical areas where I and other product leaders on the team usually collaborate with the CTO and senior engineering leaders:

- **Defining roadmap**: working together with the CTO and engineering leads to revise the roadmap quarterly (an activity owned by product leaders with involvement from the CTO function). This includes collaborative delivery planning for key projects and revision of the tech debt.
- **R&D agile processes and CI/CD pipeline**: analyzing bottlenecks and planning activities for improvement (owned by the CTO and his team; senior product leaders raise top issues and participate in generating solutions).
- **Troubleshooting team performance**: collaborating with teams that are falling behind in performance to identify root causes and help the team get back on track.
- **New architecture decisions**: planning major innovations that require new technological approaches collaboratively with the CTO and senior engineering leaders.

The key to alignment between all R&D functions:

- **Involve engineering leaders in the strategic process**: make engineering leaders - from the CTO to engineering team leaders - part of the strategic process at their respective levels. They need to be involved in decision-making about goals, priorities, and roadmap changes before any commitments are made to stakeholders and customers. I.e., they should participate in key meetings with the CEO and GTM leaders where important alignment happens & decisions are made. This makes these commitments a shared responsibility, rather than solely the product leaders', and helps the engineering and delivery organizations understand the rationale.
- **Joint R&D team**: collaboration between senior product and technology leaders, joint team activities, visibility of shared KPIs (product, technical, operational) and organizational structure positioning are essential to make the teams feel part of a unified R&D team rather than multiple separate organizations.
- **Facilitate customer interactions**: the CTO, engineering, and delivery leaders should not be completely disconnected from direct interactions with customers. It is the job of product leaders to help facilitate these interactions by involving them in some customer-facing meetings and occasionally inviting customers to team activities, such as kick-offs or demos.

### 2) Examples building products through the use of AI? How has this transformed the customer experience?

**ML engine for CRM applications**

*Context:* At Creatio, as the leader of our CRM portfolio, I led the launch of an ML predictive engine designed for sales, marketing, and service automation. Our goal was to enhance the CRM's value proposition by integrating ML-based tools for efficient enterprise use. The main challenges included identifying customer problems that could be solved by ML models within real-world data constraints + ensuring the implementation fits our no-code platform vision, allowing citizen developers to customize with AI/ML without needing programming skills.

We started by piloting a few promising use cases for our existing customers and then scaled to develop a configurable engine for various business tasks, including:

- *classification tasks* (i.e. automatically routing support cases or categorizing them);
- *predictive scoring & forecasting* (i.e., lead scoring, opportunity forecasting, and budget assessment);
- *recommendations* (i.e., suggesting cross-sell products)

*Customer Impact:* These features achieved adoption rates between 10% and 30%, which is quite high for this kind of functionality. Key successes I recall include a tech company reducing first-line support costs by approximately 30%, and a retail bank implementing real-time product recommendations for account managers, boosting cross-sales revenue by up to 10%.

**Gen AI developer assistant**

*Context:* at a stealth-mode startup I'm currently working with as a fractional CPO *(Sep-2024 framing)*, we're building an LLM-powered system to help developers accelerate their work. The major capabilities of the product is the deep awareness of the entire codebase context and advanced reasoning for some industry-specific development tasks. For the early adopters who tested the product it has shown already:

- visible improvement in onboarding time for new developers who need to navigate a large project codebase and explore existing architecture;
- promising results in generating end-to-end implementation plan and code by functional requirements.
