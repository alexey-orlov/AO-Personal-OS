## Snapshot

- **The account.** ALSO (Palo Alto), Rivian's micromobility spin-out of March 2025, designs small software-defined EVs with its own electronics, firmware and batteries: the TM-B e-bike (pedal-by-wire DreamRide drivetrain), TM-Q pedal-assist quads for families and delivery fleets, the Alpha Wave helmet, and, under a multi-year DoorDash deal (2026-03) funded by a $150M Series D (2026-08), autonomous delivery vehicles. About 390 people (LinkedIn, 2026-11), about 50 of them firmware, electrical and test engineers (two sizing methods, medium confidence). Its finance ERP is Oracle NetSuite, hence the Oracle AI Packs angle beside the Jumpstart on the team's list. At SoftServe: owner Yuliia Kushnirchuk, DO Vlad Shubenkov; HIL testing ($600K) is open in CRM. - **What ALSO's Confluence documents** (Rovo digests of pages updated 2026-11-10 to 11-19; Rovo's caveat: documented concerns, not a live backlog audit, and parts of the workstream tracker are an October snapshot). Terms: an ECU is one of the vehicle's control units; the BMS is the battery management system; SIL (software-in-the-loop) runs the firmware against a simulated vehicle, HIL (hardware-in-the-loop) runs it on the real board wired to a simulated vehicle. Coyote is the bike customers already ride (likely the TM-B, inferred); Manatee is a vehicle program in bring-up (which product is unconfirmed); C and D are hardware build generations (inferred). (chat, 2026-11-24)
  - *Platform firmware.* **Security release:** secure boot works on the SR5 ECUs, but cloud signing, key provisioning, CI/CD integration and factory validation still need coordinating, and the handover notes raise one unconfirmed security concern in diagnostics access. **Owners:** the remaining security tasks have no joint schedule, STM32 secure boot has no named owner, and Versioning V2, RTOS/RAICOM and the test infrastructure have ownership gaps. **Tests:** several test frameworks coexist with no agreed consolidation; as of 11-18 full-ECU SIL testing is still open, BMS2 tests are excluded and platform tests don't block merges; host-test coverage has improved. **Next-generation platform:** CCM2 is the reference port, with much of BMS2, WTCM2 and SCM2 still to do; FreeRTOS stack-overflow monitoring is missing; a dual-core safety decision on SR5 is open. **Build and CI:** product filtering, per-ECU builds, caching and path filters are unfinished, the Bazel upgrades open, speed gains too early to claim (11-18); observability dashboards are live.
  - *Hardware, battery and manufacturing.* **Field failures with safety implications on Coyote:** loss of propulsion tied to welded BMS FETs, with no clear fix path; brake sensors that report braking when no one brakes, cutting propulsion (cases still under investigation; not all confirmed hardware faults). **Manatee's new battery hardware:** cell thermistors out of spec, reading about 4.6 °C low, so a thermistor rework is under way (accuracy, long-term drift); first BMS board tests forced board reworks. **Parts and test readiness:** vehicle signal harnesses wait on the supplier, battery analog-front-end testing had not started, charging and test equipment was still being set up; the BMS boards, the fixture and the HIL harnesses have arrived, and packs are due in time for first integration. **Open hardware–firmware interface decisions:** pre-charge temperature protection, charger behaviour, shutdown and comms-loss conditions, which battery signals go on the main and which on the auxiliary CAN bus. **Manufacturing and service:** low generator-stator yield; PCM board arrival a risk to the D build; production qualification waits on firmware; in the field, mixed C and D modules and security pairing complicate part replacements, and service tools lag.
  - *Product firmware (the ECUs' own code).* **Coyote power faults:** besides the loss of propulsion, reserve-battery charging problems, CAN going offline at low charge and no charging from 0%; the planned firmware release fixes only part of the set, and the investigations are cross-team, not code alone. **Fault response:** Manatee's diagnostic review finds the shared fault framework mostly reports faults without triggering the intended protective responses; pre-charge fault gating and wake-up inhibition are unfinished (hardware safety paths and the BMS control model's protections are separate). **Tests:** the same CI gaps, plus gaps in controller-application and CAN integration testing in Manatee's test strategy (an October baseline). **Firmware, Controls and Systems boundaries (Manatee):** power and vehicle-state policy, fault recovery, safe stop vs immediate torque-off and calibration ownership are undecided; the ownership-split page is a proposal, not an agreement, and notes torque commands forwarded once a second where 10 ms is required. **Release regressions:** a security change removed a capability needed to replace a drive unit in the field (fixed in main, not yet released), so factory firmware, service tooling and mixed C/D modules must be validated together.
- **The documentation is itself a problem.** Plain Confluence search surfaced none of this; only Rovo's synthesis did, because the documentation has no structure (Alex, 2026-11-24).
- **Where the offer's directions meet these problems** (inferred until tested with the account team; the hardware deck's own units, direct matches only, as in the R&D explainer's mapping, OneDrive `Current offerings/AI Jumpstart for Hardware & Embedded R&D/Explainer/research/jumpstart-mapping.md`). *01 Hardware-in-the-loop development*, example module *Peripheral bring-up*: the boards in bring-up, Manatee's BMS boards and the BMS2, WTCM2 and SCM2 ports after CCM2. *02 Legacy code modernization*, its "Adding tests to untested C code": the controller-application test gaps. No direction targets the rest, which is most of what the documents call the problem: decisions and owners across teams (security release, the hardware–firmware interface, firmware vs controls), fault-response dispatch, full-ECU SIL and CAN integration testing, build and CI speed, field-fault triage and unsearchable documentation. The open HIL testing ask ($600K) has no shipped module either: the tracker pitches it as the follow-on once Bench and HIL test automation ships.
