**Focus of the program (Alex's open question 2)**
- Alex put three options: (a) the SDLC-Jumpstart pattern, modeling one researcher's pipeline and accelerating its steps with agents, skills and integrations; (b) the PM-track pattern, adding team collaboration and a shared knowledge layer; (c) platform-led, picking one or two flagship science platforms and implementing them.
- Speaker A: combine (b)'s two strengths, upskilling the team and knowledge as the foundation, and layer **verticalized skills for scientists** on their lifecycle. Three rough steps: form a research hypothesis, gather what is needed to test it, test it in the lab. A fourth step, manufacturing and production optimization (digital twin), is reachable but not the focus.
- **Platforms: yes, but only those that can give leads**: Microsoft Discovery, Claude Science, OpenAI's science workbench, and NVIDIA (AI-Q for research, open-source drug-discovery models; covers elements, not the whole process; whether it covers the platform layer is TBD). That is three closed-source and one open-source, and the arsenal needs both. AWS and Google maybe, to research.
- **Not NobleAI-type partners.** A saw NobleAI's demo and offering: vibe-coded, weak software, its only strength is domain knowledge. It is aggressive on forward-deployed engineering, covering the AI layer itself and leaving partners cloud and program management. The aim is to displace what NobleAI does for clients, e.g. alongside Microsoft Discovery.
- Inna: Microsoft is the most relevant partner, with a high partnership level and requests already coming from its partner team; Anthropic and OpenAI won't hand over leads yet. A: Microsoft Discovery has an established partnership and a sales machine that sells funded deals into deep enterprises.
- Alex: lead generation need not come from the vendors. On the Jumpstarts it ran from the client's own stack (a client on Claude gets a Claude build). A agreed demand came from clients, so the program should be positioned as tied to no single partner, which keeps the direct route to clients open.

**Level of responsibility (Alex's open question 3)**
- Alex's framing: (a) an upskilling program, with KPIs and baseline measured but SoftServe's responsibility ending at training, vs (b) a transformation program that rebuilds workflows, roles and how departments interact. His Payworks experience: client success only comes with (b), SoftServe didn't do (b) there, and that trade-off puts the client's long-term success in question.
- Speaker A: **always sell (b), but let the client buy a package**, e.g. five months of implementation, or a pricier package that changes the whole organization. Historically SoftServe "did the first and sold the second", learning the second along the way, with some cases where it delivered the second (Inna: still learning). The market moves faster than the team learns.
- NobleAI's value proposition as a starting model: a 5-month engagement that rolls out a scientific AI platform on target areas, sets the target in month 1 and delivers something working by month 5.
- First use-case insights: an agent that researches the sources, speeds up citation and adds nuance; an agent that operates lab hardware to run trials. No exact skills or workflows yet; they get built creatively in the first engagements.
