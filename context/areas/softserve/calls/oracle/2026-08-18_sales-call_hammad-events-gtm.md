## Hammad's remit — and why it matters

Oracle's team splits into **demand** and **supply**. Hammad is on **demand generation**, covering:

- **AIDP and AI Lakehouse** — explicitly named as the two big initiatives he needs to boost demand for
- **Generative AI services**
- **Dedicated AI clusters** — client-committed, client-paid GPU capacity
- **Accelerators**

**Key structural insight he volunteered:** accelerator-type projects tend to land as **dedicated AI clusters**. That is Oracle's monetisation path out of the accelerator motion, and it tells us where his incentives sit — **he is measured on pipeline into AIDP / Lakehouse / GPU commit, not on services revenue.**

Our pitch to him should always be framed as *"this removes friction on your path to a client commitment"* — exactly how he framed it back:

> "For us it's all about removing any frictions for people to get to pilots. For you it's about showing your capabilities, deeper client relationship, going into production and making real money."

A clean, honest alignment statement — **reuse it internally.**

## The three agreed tracks

### Track 1 — Participation (lowest friction, agreed in principle)

- We attend as participants. **Hammad can register us**; there will be a landing page for Lakehouse Summit.
- His advice: **don't chase the AI Experience labs.** *"If you're attending a lab, you're there to do hands-on stuff, not to listen — there'll be a lot of instructions being shouted."* Correct read.
- **Target the panels.** At AI Experience the roundtable is the only listening format and it's CXO-level. At Lakehouse Summit he **offered stage time outright**.
- **Action for us:** pick the cities that matter and put a **named person** against each.

### Track 2 — Content

- Oracle runs the labs. What they want from a partner is **field proof**: customer stories, implementation showcases, "what we actually saw."
- Realistic format he validated: a **5–10 minute partner showcase** inside a session someone else runs, or panel contribution.
- **Our qualifier, stated on the call:** for the 16 AI Experience events, content contribution is hard until we have a **specific, nameable customer story**. For Lakehouse we have more to say.
- **Alex's constructive counter, which Hammad accepted as "an area":** we don't need a completed at-scale AIDP implementation to have content. We can **pre-package 4–5 AIDP use cases** — descriptions plus lightweight demos — deliberately chosen against **where Oracle sees demand emerging**, and speak them in value language at the events. **This requires Hammad to nominate the 4–5 demand areas. That is our single most important ask of him.**

### Track 3 — Implementation / post-event conversion (the commercial core)

**The problem Hammad articulated clearly:** a client does a live lab, hears something good, has a problem — and then nothing happens, because *"people are very weary of doing pilots. Either they don't want to pay, or they want to pay very little."*

**His proposed fix, unprompted**, from notes of our earlier call with Neil: put a **low-cost, fixed-duration SoftServe engagement** in front of the client at the event. *"Here's a package SoftServe can pursue — if you're interested, reach out to these people and we'll do a 30-day engagement at very low cost for you to explore how to solve that problem."*

**Where we actually stand, as clarified on the call:**

- **Bosch** (workforce optimisation) was **60 days, not 30** — more complex use case. **Not AIDP.**
- Packages in the **Oracle–SoftServe Business Alignment deck** he had open: **~$60K and ~$100K tiers**. He found and referenced this deck himself, including the practice-setup and investments section — **he is reading our material. Assume anything we send him gets read and quoted back.**
- **Accelerator-based engagements can go faster than 60 days**; the accelerator scope is being finalised now. **These are not AIDP.**
- **AIDP is where we are putting our bet.** **Gero's** stated managed-service execution timeline is also **30 days**, which gives us a natural shape: a **joint SoftServe + Gero + Oracle 30-day AIDP entry package**, used as the event instrument, with a defined handover into scale.
- **Hard constraint we flagged to him: no pricing commitment until it clears our internal profitability review.** We can bring shape and ideas to the next meeting; **not numbers**.

## Insights & implications

- **Re-target our event effort.** We had been planning around a large number of AI lab events as the channel. The real prize is **two Lakehouse Summits where we can be on stage in front of CXOs**, plus **selective panel appearances** across the 16 AI Experience cities. Concentrate effort accordingly — and note **London is one of the two Summit cities**, a home-turf advantage.
- **The AIDP-off-the-lab-agenda move is a signal worth watching.** If AIDP loses lab presence while Lakehouse keeps it, **Oracle's own internal centre of gravity for hands-on enablement is Lakehouse**. Our AIDP bet is still right on the demand side (Hammad owns AIDP demand), but **our event content should lead with Lakehouse and carry AIDP as the forward-looking layer**.
- **Hammad thinks in packages, not capabilities.** Every time we described capability, he reframed it as *"what do I hand a client at the end of a session."* Our deliverables to him should be **one-page, priced-shaped, named, with a routing contact**.
- **We have an attribution problem to manage.** An Oracle product manager (ex-AWS) told the team he had used SoftServe before, and **asked for a reference that didn't come back**. We corrected the record: the AWS partnership is US-based and run by a different cloud team; our R&D team is Oracle-focused; the adjacent work was **Qualcomm / small-language-model with AWS as substrate** (migration/modernisation). **Follow-up worth doing:** find out who that PM is and get him a proper reference — **an unanswered internal reference request at Oracle is a slow leak on our credibility**.
- **The 60 vs 30 day gap is a live commercial question.** Hammad is anchored on **30 days at low cost**. Our proven artefact is **60 days at $60–100K**. We need to decide deliberately whether we **build a genuine 30-day AIDP entry package** (and what we cut to get there) or **reset his anchor**. **Do not let this be settled by default in a meeting.**
- **Process risk:** almost everything agreed here is **verbal** and rests on Hammad personally locating agendas and making introductions. **Get the asks in writing before he travels.**
