## Key points
- Background: SoftServe built two NVIDIA-based applications with Oracle; wants to list them and later package solutions beyond NVIDIA (e.g. AIDP). AJ framed Marketplace as a logical distribution point and a gateway to follow-on services.
- Tony/TPX has been on Marketplace ~2 years; TPX was acquired by TPX Impact ~4.5 years ago; his work is Oracle-centric.
- Free listings are easy to register; paid listings are the harder path.
- TPX keeps paid listings private because each customer gets a different deal, SOW, terms and rates. Public listings only make sense for a generic, self-installable app with uniform rates.
- Main value of Marketplace for TPX: bypassing lengthy procurement (a bank could take 6 months) and letting customers burn expiring Oracle cloud credits. Examples: a $125/hour VM listing to burn year-end credits; a generic public POC/pilot listing at $35/hour.
- Mechanism: a VM image carrying TPX software is deployed in the customer tenancy; while the machine runs, TPX bills hourly for IP + services (managed-service style). Description of services on registration can carry the SOW; TPX paid a few thousand pounds for its own legal T&Cs.
- Three transaction models identified by AJ: (1) private offer with SOW — full amount paid in advance, no bundled service margin; (2) ISV/product listing with margin on product; (3) plain VM image with an hourly rate burning credits (services only). TPX has used only the VM method, considered the private offer.
- Private offer is preferable for multi-year or complex deals: payment upfront, minus ~3% Oracle fee, not dependent on credit renewal. VM method pays monthly in arrears (payment typically within one to two months).
- Credit rule: customers can typically spend only 25% of credits on Marketplace; overage charges apply above that. Exceptions are commonly granted — Oracle uses an internal Deal Approval System (DAS); this applies to both consumption and private offers.
- Marketplace team support: minimal GTM support, essentially technical/backend only. Team is resource-constrained, West Coast based, hard to reach. Tony's example: two weeks with no answer on US Government Cloud publishing.
- Contacts: generic Marketplace support email (Tony shared in chat) is the starting point; Roger Donaldson is the director and decision-maker but very hard to reach (tactic: 7:30 AM PT meeting invites); Federico Lopez reports to Angelica Martinez and is now improving in responsiveness. AJ confirmed Roger said he is resource constrained and has a new second-in-command, Colin.
- Federico: Marketplace motion is established in North America/Montevideo but new to EMEA; small team, relies on NA resources.
- Multicloud: Marketplace is OCI only. Tony had a listing rejected for mentioning Azure. AIDP is an Oracle product, not an ISV listing — services around it would go via a private offer.
- US entity requirement: payouts require a US partner entity with a US bank account; Oracle pays in USD to avoid cross-border tax complexity. SoftServe confirmed it has a US operation. Oracle uses constant-currency/hedging rates rather than spot rates.
- Packaging: Terraform, VM image or Kubernetes container are all options; TPX only uses VM images because customer setups differ. Publisher tenancy must have an OCI region in Ashburn — SoftServe is setting this up.
- SoftServe's concern that publishing requires provisioning specific GPUs in its own tenancy: Tony has never encountered this (GPUs are enabled in the customer tenancy). AJ advised packaging against on-demand GPU capacity for pilots and swapping to a dedicated cluster for production — likely just an endpoint change.
