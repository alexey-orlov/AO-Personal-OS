## TL;DR
- Oracle's OCI AI product team (Eric Rothrock, Sri Sabbella) wants to put its effort into platform/PaaS services such as managed inference and model serving. It does not want to build solutions. It is keeping AI Accelerator Packs to the three certified blueprints (AIQ, cuOpt, VSS) until they bring in real revenue, meaning millions or more.
- Both sides agreed on the split: SoftServe builds and owns the solution layer and sells repeatable packs on OCI Marketplace (Terraform or VM image) for use in customer tenancies. Oracle keeps the base templates and the NVIDIA blueprint versions up to date.
- Eric has no roadmap beyond the packs already published. He first wants market feedback on how SoftServe uses them and the blockers it hits. He'll consider investing more only if there's a strong business case.

## Key points
- **Why the call:** Pankaj Sharma organised it so SoftServe could meet the OCI AI product team and align on the roadmap, what each side expects, and blockers. He noted this team is not the incubation team SoftServe dealt with before (Pulkit, around July).
- **Customer momentum:** Some customers are in production and some are in pilot. Sky, Bosch, NHS and Riyadh Air were named. SoftServe is running 8 engagements with the European team at different stages.
- **Oracle's position (Eric):**
  - OCI should own the PaaS layer: above the GPUs and below the solutions.
  - Engineering resources are tightly controlled. Only small tweaks are possible without a strong ROI case.
  - Inference work pays back better than the accelerators do right now.
  - He wants OCI to offer something that makes a joint solution run on OCI rather than on AWS or Azure.
- **Sri:** Oracle wants customer signal on which packs get traction, so it can prioritise limited resources and justify spending on them.
- **SoftServe's position (Bohdan Khomych, Oleksii Orlov):**
  - SoftServe wants to be the solution provider, building packs from each engagement to reuse with other customers (for example, workforce optimisation from Bosch). It would take on the work Oracle sees demand for but has no capacity to build.
  - Its reliance on the current packs is low. Some projects use only the UI surface, some go deeper, and some presales work uses plain OCI plus NVIDIA blueprints with no pack at all.
  - Improvements to the platform layer (AIQ, cuOpt) are mostly NVIDIA's call, not Oracle's.
- **AJ (Adrian James):**
  - cuOpt stays generic. SoftServe customises it per use case and packages the result for Marketplace.
  - Customers start on on-demand consumption and later move to a DAC (expanded as Dedicated Region/AI Cloud (inferred)).
  - Oracle only needs to step in when NVIDIA releases a new blueprint version that has to be brought onto the platform.
  - A move into Omniverse would only follow once enough demand shows up.
- **Eric's team scope:** managed inference, the serving stack, commercial and open-weight models, guardrails and similar tooling, and partner offerings (for example OpenAI).
- **NVIDIA vs Oracle-native services:** Oleksii asked whether Oracle-native AI services could replace NVIDIA components. AJ said yes: the blueprints allow any NVIDIA part to be swapped for Oracle services, and customers often already have contracts for those services. Pankaj and Alex Negrea can advise on this.

## Decisions & commitments
- Oracle (Eric, Sri): no new packs beyond AIQ, cuOpt and VSS until revenue justifies it. Will keep the base packages updated when SoftServe flags a need. No date.
- SoftServe: will package the customised solutions (Bosch, Sky, Riyadh Air and others) as Marketplace offerings. Already in progress with AJ. No date.
- SoftServe: will share what it learns from current engagements, including pack usage and blockers. No date.
- Pankaj Sharma: will schedule a follow-up in about 1 to 1.5 months to review production progress and real blockers. Cadence (monthly or quarterly) to be set after that call.
- Eric and Sri: both will be points of contact on email, and either will reply. Questions from SoftServe go through Pankaj or email.
