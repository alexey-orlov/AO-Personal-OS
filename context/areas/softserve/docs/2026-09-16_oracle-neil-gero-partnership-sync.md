## 1. Headline takeaways (from the notes doc)

1. **Oracle has moved from strategy to execution.** Thirteen to sixteen "laser campaigns" are now launched — targeted sales plays built around AI Lakehouse, AI Data Platform (AIDP) or OCI, engineered for in-quarter pipeline and same-FY bookings. Oracle is operationalising them country by country. This is the window for SoftServe to be the named delivery partner.
2. **The single biggest ask on us is commercial packaging, not technical capability.** Neil wants T-shirt-sized (S/M/L) price points he can hand to the field: pilot = X, pilot-to-production = Y, full production = Z, scaled by users / pipelines / storage. Without this, SoftServe is not "ready to go" in the field's eyes.
3. **A joint pipeline call starts next week**, owned by Hamad with Ege as point person. First two or three calls to be used to build the UK campaign rather than review pipe.
4. **The Bosch case, not Sky, is our most globally repeatable asset.** Bosch is already packaged on cost, price and timeline and is generalised for redeployment; it needs no EV regulation hook and ties to base Fusion functionality, so any Fusion customer with fleet optimisation need qualifies. Sky remains the better UK-specific story.
5. **"POC as door opener" is now validated.** Riyadh Air, Saudi Binladin Group, Bosch and Sky are all generating follow-on use case demand. We should attach a parallel Oracle + SoftServe AI discovery/ideation workshop to every POC as standard practice.
6. **AIDP: adopt, don't build.** Oracle's advice is to build IP understanding around the ~34 newly released EBS agents and semantic engines rather than construct accelerators from scratch. Do not bundle bare-metal GPU/NVIDIA cases into AIDP — too early.

## 2. Oracle go-to-market machine — what changed

- **Laser campaigns:** thirteen launched (sixteen referenced later in the call), each a targeted play on AI Lakehouse, AIDP or OCI, designed for in-quarter pipeline with above-average conversion this financial year. Reception has been strong; now being operationalised at country level. Gero to share the field training deck (contains revenue figures, cleared for sharing with Bohdan, Olia and Karsten).
- **Gold Mines:** roughly 1,000 target EMEA accounts loaded into the CRM tool. Every Oracle rep is measured on whether they reviewed the Gold Mine idea, pitched it, and generated a workshop and follow-on. This is where our pipeline will originate.
- **Capacity reality check:** Oracle is going through a round of redundancies. Every team has lost people and is absorbing extra workload. Expect slower Oracle-side response times and design our plays to need minimal Oracle effort.

**Engagement model — where SoftServe enters.** Oracle reps pitch, then Oracle pre-sales demo the live stack (AI Lakehouse, AI Life Hub, AIDP). When the customer wants to experiment, that is the earliest reasonable insertion point for SoftServe — still pre-sales. We then carry solution design and architecture, and the proposal stage. Neil was explicit that he does not want to load us with work that will not convert.

**Deal economics.**

| Play | Oracle deal size | Cycle | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI Lakehouse / AI Life Hub | ~$65K–$200K | Short | High volume, factory approach |
| AIDP | $1M+ | Longer | Consultative: solution design, architecture, pilots |

Oracle will lean in with its own experts on our **first** AI Lakehouse delivery to de-risk it; subsequent engagements should need far less support.

## 5. AIDP

**Question.** GPU incentives for Oracle sales teams appear to have shifted toward the platform and AIDP level. Is it worth SoftServe building accelerators on AIDP the way we did with the Oracle AI packs — and can we work with Oracle product management as we did before?

**Neil.** Yes, but adopt rather than build. There are roughly half a dozen AIDP use cases. The strongest is the 34 agents just released for EBS: the EBS install base is large and cannot move to AI without migrating to Fusion or another SaaS product, so "deploy AIDP plus these 34 agents" is a contained, sellable accelerator. Build IP understanding rather than full accelerators. Do not drop AI Lakehouse and Life Hub — smaller deals but higher volume and a genuine factory motion.

**Gero.** Do not combine bare-metal GPU/NVIDIA cases with AIDP — too early. Traction is in EBS agents; he will send the list of agents becoming available, covering finance and transaction use cases. The other major AIDP push is **semantic engines**. Step zero is onboarding SoftServe on these; Helen is the right person and Gero has already asked her to do the same for his own team.

**Product management access.** Helen (London) and Oscar (Madrid) are the local PMs and both will be introduced. The real dependency is the AIDP product team in North America (Santhosh). Neil was candid that even he and Gero struggle with that team, as they tend to act independently without communicating. Start local, then work the NA introduction jointly.

**GPU sales rules — clarified.** No one in Oracle can sell raw GPUs; a central global team handles that and it effectively requires Larry Ellison's personal sign-off, because supply is scarce (the first shipment of 125,000 NVIDIA GPUs was fully allocated before delivery). The field **can** sell dedicated AI clusters — public cloud, 4/6/8/12 nodes, typically older GPUs. That is exactly what Sky runs on. Position accordingly: never propose raw GPU procurement, always dedicated AI clusters.
