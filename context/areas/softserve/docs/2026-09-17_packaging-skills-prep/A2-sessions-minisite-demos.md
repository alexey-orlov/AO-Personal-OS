## 3. Consolidated rulebook for the skills

### 3a · Mini-site product listing

1. **Persona first, in the reader's words.** Every headline and lead is written from the seat of (a) an Oracle/partner rep opening the page live on a call and (b) an enterprise buyer reading alone. Test: would the rep say it out loud? *(§18.7: "You overemphasize the counts and the scaffolding, not the essence.")*
2. **No counts, no taxonomy, no packaging vocabulary in copy.** "seven products", "three workflow patterns", "packaged", "ready-to-run", "accelerator pack", "pods", "scoped", "evaluation-first" are internal. A number is a headline only when the number is the reader's information (a price, a duration). *(§18.7, §18.9)*
3. **Never state a ceiling, a total, a denominator or a negation.** No "5 of 7", no zero-count facet, no "not seeing your workflow?", no "so far"/"yet". State breadth positively and only where cleared. *(§18.9a1)*
4. **One-liners state the job and the outcome, never the packaging.** *(S1 1.13)* Carry two lengths: a full `oneLiner` and a rep-sayable `shortLine`.
5. **Heading budgets, measured on the rendered page:** H1 2–4 words (≤~24 chars/line, ≤2 lines), H2 ≤5 words (≤~30 chars); the argument goes in the lead; verify the phone break. *(S3 3.3)*
6. **Structure before copy:** audience → positioning distinct from the sibling page → 3–4 messages each answering a reader problem → one screen per message → length target set up front. Cut any block carrying no message. *(S3 3.2, S1 1.9)*
7. **Repetition limits:** no content word three times on one screen; one word for one thing across the whole piece; no claim in more than two places. *(§18.7/18.8 copy-edit passes)*
8. **One component grammar across every listing**, enforced by a checker; each information type has one fixed visual form (verticals get icons, features get lists, metrics get tiles). Absence renders as an empty container, never a dead control. *(S1 1.3, 1.4)*
9. **Overview block order is fixed and priority-ordered:** problem↔solution → features as workflow steps *with a frame per step* → vertical use cases as a tabbed component → ROI metrics in a side rail. *(S1 1.7)*
10. **Architecture ≠ capability.** Technology = an architecture layer diagram (with per-item required/optional flags) + a capability list grouped by workflow stage. "How it runs" never gets its own block. *(S1 1.8, 1.14)*
11. **The packages block sells one idea** — pilot fast, low risk, tangible outputs. Deliverables are restated as customer outcomes. Name it *Jumpstart* / *Jumpstart Proof-of-Value*. *(S1 1.15)*
12. **Price discipline:** publish only the PoV price; other tiers read "Scoped per engagement"; a price never ships without its disclaimer; no € figures on the services page. *(Alex's brief; still violated by two products — an open item)*
13. **Commitments are enforced, not written:** one duration (4–8 weeks) everywhere, asserted by the checker. A duration, a price or a promise is a commitment, not copy. *(S3 3.5)*
14. **No customer names or logos in anything shipped** — copy, alt text, captions, data files, or unreferenced files under the deployable root. Use an anonymized descriptor (industry + scale, nothing that back-solves) plus an industry medallion. Clearance is revocable; re-check every round. *(S2 2.4, §17.0, §25.4)*
15. **Status said once, in one plain word** (proven / forecast / estimated / measured / modeled / in preparation), with a footnote that spends its line on evidence. *(§18.9a2)*
16. **Canonical vocabulary per axis.** The platform chip shows the facet's canonical vendor product name; engine specifics live on the Technology tab. Get the vendor's own naming right. *(S2 2.5)*
17. **Three tag families, three visual codes**, each with an icon and tooltip: workflow pattern (outlined) · platform (solid navy = fact) · availability (teal badge: demo exists / on marketplace). Availability is a capability, not a lifecycle state. *(S2 2.2–2.3)*
18. **Owner-controlled order** via a config parameter; equal-size tiles; one CTA per tile; legibility over imagery. *(S1 1.11–1.12)*
19. **Imagery only in the identity block**, sourced from the company's own deck corpus (never the web); body tabs are diagrammatic. *(S1 1.1–1.2)*
20. **Contact = a named person + a shared practice mailbox**, card one side / form the other. Never a personal mailbox. *(S1 1.10)*
21. **Seller materials are requested, not listed:** gate on the corporate email domain (never a role field), promise the outcome, route the ineligible somewhere real, and only claim "we've emailed" when something actually sends. *(S3 3.6; START-HERE §4 truthful confirmations)*
22. **Ship the prototype with its own unconfirmed-assumptions checklist** — one tickable line per item, ticks in the lightest storage that works; the analysis lives in the docs. *(S3 3.8)*
23. **The owner's statements about his own pipeline outrank a QA finding.** A finding may change the phrasing, never the existence. *(§17.6)*

### 3b · Interactive demo

1. **Keep the real product's flow, screens and information model; generalize the content** to what the pack sells (S/M/L rows + feature matrix), never to the one customer it was built on. Where no product exists, the "real product" is the platform — interfaces must be recognizable, not invented. *(brief; playbook 1)*
2. **Brand-agnostic and industry-neutral on synthetic data with ground-truth specifics.** No *customer* marks (name, logo, geography, ids). Vendor/platform marks are expected where the demo runs on that platform. No uncleared figure; no time-to-deliver claim unless cleared. *(brief; playbook 2)*
3. **No integrations, no real inputs** — upload, export and write-back mocked elegantly. A short guided flow (six steps), hints that allow only the designated control, free exploration afterwards, and a cleaner UI than the real product. *(brief; playbook 3)*
4. **Deliberately simplify a tedious real workflow** for demo purposes. *(brief)*
5. **Red-team the first cut against the pack specs** before delivering; widen without changing the flow; report the delta as a TLDR. *(5.4)*
6. **Lead with the value, not the screens.** Before → after on the product's own KPIs in one band, immediately after the run; then drill down from each number to the change that produced it (rule + effect); then let the viewer override by hand and watch the numbers recompute. *(WfO round 2)*
7. **A dedicated passive tour step pauses on the improved metrics** (Next control + a guard that blocks every other click), then a step that opens where the gain comes from. *(playbook 6)*
8. **The AI must be seen reasoning at every step**, on an AI-native business task with an outcome promise. Heavy AI work behind a progress bar reads as ETL. Pick a decision with money on it — not a hygiene task; the promise is an outcome, never hours saved. Hint titles are business sentences in the persona's words; no hint says refresh, model, mapping, view or SQL outside Explain/Trace. Open on the integrated sources, not a job run. *(ERP Q&A round 2)*
9. **Interface fidelity is checkable.** Audit every element against a real reference corpus, classify A (deviation with a reference) / B (invention with none) / C (match), fix the A-items, and **report every residual invention with its reason**. Measure with `getComputedStyle`/`getBoundingClientRect`, not by eye. *(ERP Q&A round 3: "I expect a full match")*
10. **The end card is for the viewer** — what they can act on now, three sentences, three "try next" doors, no figures; never a recap of what the build did. **The counter must move on every click** — show position inside the step ("Step 4 of 6 · 2 of 3"). *(ERP Q&A round 2)*
11. **Word budgets everywhere:** gate body ≤40 words in two sentences + one line for step count and time; hint titles ≤6 words, bodies ≤35; end card ≤30; toasts ≤15; card descriptions/helpers ≤14; footers one line. *(ERP Q&A round 4)*
12. **Data model = current state + named changes with additive KPI effects.** Flags are a function of what is applied; KPIs computed in the page so every number reconciles and an undo recomputes them. Compute deltas from raw means, not rounded displays. *(playbook 4)*
13. **Figures:** reproduce the cleared figure; companions synthetic and modest; the shipped stills show the state that carries the cleared figure; **list every synthetic figure for Alex in the report.** *(playbook 7)*
14. **Demo captures become the listing's step frames and poster**, and the listing gets a secondary CTA to the demo opening in a new tab. *(brief)*
15. **Hosting:** publish the demo as its own artifact and link it via `demoPreviewUrl`; an artifact's supporting file cannot open as a top-level page. Keep the relative path canonical for the real deployment. *(5.3)*
16. **QA by scripted click-through** (`tools/capture-demo-frames.mjs` `MODE=script` + a scenario JSON); `LOGS: none` is the gate; then look at the shots.

### 3c · Cross-cutting

**Messaging & naming.** Persona-first; internal vocabulary never ships; the pack name is one owner-set string used identically everywhere; vendor product names verified against the vendor's own label; the site/app is named for what it offers, not the org unit; a name built on a vendor trademark needs a usage-guidelines check before launch.

**Data clearance (the single most-repeated theme).**
- *Cleared to publish*: figures already printed on external one-pagers, in the external-safe tier; the practice mailbox; the named contact's title as printed verbatim on the one-pagers; SoftServe's own public positioning sentences and counters (he explicitly asked for these instead of invented positioning).
- *Never publish*: customer names and logos (withdrawn 2026-09-16 after being granted on 09-14); figures from an Oracle-copyright, "Confidential: Internal/Restricted" deck without both Oracle and customer sign-off; internal deck filenames or internal paths in any publicly fetchable file (e.g. an image manifest); package prices beyond PoV; internal vocabulary; the "gap" or the roadmap ceiling.
- *Must stay synthetic in a demo*: geography, ids, names, documents, companions to a cleared headline figure; every synthetic figure listed for Alex.
- *Mechanics*: a word-boundary deny-list in the checker for every customer name; a grep gate before publish; **anything that must never ship lives outside the deployable root** ("unreferenced is not unshipped" — logos sat unreferenced under `site/assets/img/logos/` and were still downloadable by path from the link-shared artifact until v36).

**Design.** Dark ground + one accent per screen; display type vs light body; at most one light band per page; peers equal height; pill semantics (filled navy = fact, outlined = filter, teal badge = availability); an address is a link, never a filled button; a filled button is the screen's one ask; photo only in the top block; clean at 375, H1 holds at 320; no emoji; check horizontal overflow at every width.

**Process / QA / publish.** Brief (Opus labels every fact `[site]` / `[pub]` / `[clr]`) → decide (one compact Fable pass, no fan-out) → build (Opus) → checker + console + deny-list grep, all three green → look at every changed screen at 1440/1280/1024/768/375 → publish → `list_files` to confirm what is live → record in PROVENANCE → rewrite START-HERE where a rule moved → report what changed, what was decided differently and why, and what is still open. **Turn every new owner rule into a checker assertion so it survives the next rewrite.**

**Model routing (Alex's own words).** "All smart, creative, important decisions, tailored messaging, layout to be developed by Fable, tedious part to Opus." Fable: messaging, UX decisions, the data model where numbers must reconcile, the red-team pass, the final review. Opus: research, extraction, builds from a spec, QA loops, captures, conversions, doc mechanics. Fable budget ~7% of its limit, 10% hard cap. Fable's copy always gets an Opus pass before it ships (HTML entities, retired vocabulary creeping back, leads running long).

**Inputs Alex supplies** (HANDOFF §7): demo videos and posters, Marketplace URLs, success stories, kit links, the form endpoint, the hosting subdomain, customer-name approvals, image rights. **What he wants before approving:** the live page/demo to click through himself (he reviews the running thing, not a description), a TLDR of what changed, the list of synthetic figures, and the decisions that remain his. He never approves silently — he returns a numbered list.

**Handoff discipline.** Before a build that needs machine-local material: distil it into the repo, write a self-contained handoff (the ask, what's in the repo, what's local + the workaround, the ordered plan, pitfalls already paid for, definition of done) and a paste-ready prompt carrying only the *next* task.
