## Snapshot

- **Program shape (Bohdan's answer to Alex's open questions):** the PM-track model (upskilling + knowledge as the foundation) with **verticalized skills over the scientist's lifecycle**: form a hypothesis → gather what the test needs → test it in the lab. Manufacturing and production optimization (digital twin) is reachable but not the focus. [sync](calls/jumpstart-science/2026-10-19_201609_default_pasted-science-jumpstart-sync.md)
- **Delivery design (exec summary page 4, 2026-10-20), headed by Alex's seven principles:**
  1. **Admit uncertainty.** The program's shape will change after the first 2–3 engagements.
  2. **Two modes.**
     - **Light (training)** fits passive clients who keep data and systems closed and whose org isn't ready to change. It enables power users and gives management the tools and a path. It runs 1 week of prep + 6 weeks.
     - **Full (transformation)** fits active clients who open data and accept integrations, with an org ready to change. It enables the entire team. It runs 2 weeks of prep + 11 weeks.
  3. **Extended discovery.** Weeks 1–3 in Full. Per sub-team it covers workflows, roles, data, systems, timelines, bottlenecks, and each person's AI use; heavy modules get a deep-dive.
  4. **Deep tailoring.** Clients pick modules from the six buckets, and each module is shaped around their specific problem. The foundation, bucket 6 (shared knowledge base and skill set), always comes first.
  5. **Three weeks per module.** SoftServe and the champions build in week N-1; show & test with a home assignment on real work in week N; participants show results in week N+1. Heavy modules (instruments, compute) build longer.
  6. **Home assignments, a demo day and an AI acceleration award** for the team member with the biggest measured speed-up, in week 11 (gamification).
  7. **Advisory after.** Up to 4 weeks (weeks 12–15), then the next-steps workshop (week 16).

- **Gates, client time and SoftServe team (page 4, 2026-10-20):** before week 1 the team is named, systems inventoried and access requested; by the end of week 3 every module has an owner, a baseline, a target and open or exported data; every week the sponsor gets a one-page status and a blocked module swaps with the next one; in week 11 the champions run the setup alone. Client hours a week, Light / Full: sponsor 1 / 1 · champions (one per sub-team) 4 / 6 · power users (5–10, home assignments) 3 / 3 · whole team 1 / 2 (Full adds assignments) · system owners 1 / 2. SoftServe team, Alex's five roles, FTE Light / Full: engagement lead (coordinates the engagement) 0.25 / 0.5 · solution & product architect (the client's architecture decisions, aligned with productizing the program) 0.5 / 0.75 · business analyst (client requirements and alignment) 0.5 / 1 · AI builders (skills, agents, integrations, RAG pipelines) 1 / 2.5 · SME (reads client data, validates outputs before the client sees them) 0.25 / 0.5; in all 2.5 / 5.25 FTE or about 17 / 64 person-weeks including prep and advisory: planning estimates for one team of 20–40. - **What the program measures (page 1, 2026-10-20).** One outcome per bucket, the same teams before vs after, counted from records the team already keeps.
  - **Why the set was redone:** Alex red-teamed the first set against his criteria: measurable; clearly moved by the program; not vanity; a direct business outcome (mostly efficiency: speed, resources, delay, experimentation velocity); clear without context; typical for the bucket.
  - **The six metrics:**
    1. Days to complete a literature or patent review (project tracker).
    2. Days from a result to the decision on what to run next (ELN).
    3. Days from a finished run to a checked result (LIMS).
    4. Days from a modeling request to its result (request log, ELN).
    5. Days to draft and sign off a disclosure, change assessment or regulator-file section (Anaqua, PLM, Veeva Vault).
    6. Weeks until a new scientist runs an experiment alone (HR start date, ELN).
  - **Dropped from the first set**, as rare, lagging, rarely logged or atypical: rework from late finds, runs redone, builds per passing design, regulator questions and change-caused failures.
  - **Bucket 2's metric was changed after Alex doubted it.** "Experiments needed to reach the target result" counted physical runs. Cheaper rounds tend to mean more runs, not fewer, so the count could rise while the work improved. The metric now times the decision step between a result and the next run instead.
