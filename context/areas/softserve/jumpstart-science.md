## Snapshot

- **What it is:** the third Jumpstart track after SDLC (engineering) and PM, for research teams, on the same enablement model: team collaboration · a shared knowledge layer · shared skills and sub-agents that, with MCP integrations, form pipelines that automate the work. Offering-design history for the other tracks: [jumpstart-pm](jumpstart-pm.md). - **Target verticals (2026-10-19):** pharma and drug discovery (small and large molecules alike) · chemical manufacturing (adhesives, DuPont-type manufacturers) · energy (cut off at the recording start; named in Inna's slides and Alex's recap, inferred). One program aims to cover all three. Chosen from Bohdan's three presales as a life-science expert and from how partners (Microsoft Discovery, NVIDIA) and the competitor NobleAI pitch, not from market research. Enterprises, not ISVs. [sync](calls/jumpstart-science/2026-10-19_201609_default_pasted-science-jumpstart-sync.md) Alex's exec summary frames the market as four industry groups: the three targets fold into life sciences and materials & process, while engineered products and AI & data research cover R&D outside them. → [exec summary](../../../outputs/jumpstart-science/jumpstart-science-exec-summary.md)
- **Program shape (Bohdan's answer to Alex's open questions):** the PM-track model (upskilling + knowledge as the foundation) with **verticalized skills over the scientist's lifecycle**: form a hypothesis → gather what the test needs → test it in the lab. Manufacturing and production optimization (digital twin) is reachable but not the focus. [sync](calls/jumpstart-science/2026-10-19_201609_default_pasted-science-jumpstart-sync.md)
- **Delivery design (exec summary page 4), Alex's seven principles as revised 2026-10-21 to bridge Arek's and Oleh's input:** 1 **Two versions:** Essential (little prior AI automation on these use cases, such as agentic literature pipelines or modeling agents; data held back by an active infosec; little time to be active) runs Arek's six-week pattern, 1 week of prep + 6 weeks then office hours, and enables power users plus tools and a path for management; Extended (the opposite) runs 2 + 11 weeks then advisory and enables the entire team; hybrid setups can work. 2 **Longer discovery:** one week in Essential, three in Extended (per sub-team: workflows, roles, data, systems, timelines, bottlenecks, each person's AI use), deep-dives for heavy modules; the first engagements may run longer while the team learns. 3 **Tailor first, then settle:** clients pick modules from the six buckets (the foundation, bucket 6, first); early engagements tailor each on the go, and after 2–4 engagements the program settles into a stricter set of default scenarios. 4 **Two cadences, picked in discovery** from how the use cases split across people: A, modules in sequence (build in week N-1, show & test with a home assignment in week N, results in week N+1; heavy modules build longer) · B, Arek's parallel streams (after shared basics, each use-case group runs as its own stream to the end, with its own client people and the same or another SoftServe engineer). 5 **Three measures per bucket:** days saved (page 1, the ROI metric) plus quality and adoption (page 4); Essential times 3–5 tracer questions in weeks 1 and 6, Extended reads the team's records. 6 **Demo and award:** home assignments on real work, a demo day, and an AI acceleration award for the biggest measured speed-up. 7 **Advisory after:** up to 4 weeks, then a next-steps workshop.
