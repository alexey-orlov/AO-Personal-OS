## Hard problems (analysis §6 — the technical scope drivers, in order of difficulty)

1. **Package definitions are unstable** — same name, different scope across contracts; piling ≠ piling (bored/driven/stone); SBG-provided items invisible in sub BOQs; no identifiers. Name-based matching across ~34 packages, ~150 subcontracts, thousands of BOQ lines with SME adjudication = **the largest single effort line**. Client raised the hallucination risk themselves and accepted a human-validation loop.
2. **Cost↔schedule join unresolved:** cost is by trade/package, schedule by facility/zone/building/phase — orthogonal. **Whether P6 activities carry a package/subcontractor code is UNCONFIRMED** (Dr. Ramadan checking) — the highest-value open question; without it the join runs through BOQ + names (= problem 1 at scale). Also: substructure reports by zone, superstructure by building — request the **combined zoning+building layout** by name.
3. **Contract parsing likely enters the PoC after all** (was phase-2): package definition isn't knowable without BOQ + responsibility matrix; documents are scanned PDFs with handwritten amendments. Compromise: parse a **bounded set** — BOQ + responsibility matrix for the selected packages only.
4. **Root cause of variance is not recorded anywhere** (confirmed from cost side too) — decisions between award and final account are invisible. Findings = **inferred candidate drivers** with evidence + confidence for SME confirmation, never assertions.
5. **Provisional sums distort everything naive:** 40–60% accuracy by design; ~SAR 1.27bn of SEVEN/Town's 5.97bn contract (~21%). **Segregate provisional vs firm from day one** or project controls dismisses the findings on sight.
6. **Reporting formats differ between the two projects** — normalisation needed between PoC projects, not just across history.
