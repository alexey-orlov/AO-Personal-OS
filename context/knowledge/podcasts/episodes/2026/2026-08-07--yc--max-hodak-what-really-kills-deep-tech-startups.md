## Summary
Max Hodak argues that deep‑tech startups rarely die for lack of a good idea; they die because they can't iterate quickly enough once the company scales. Iteration speed is determined by mundane infrastructure — purchasing, budgeting, hiring, performance systems and internal software — and getting those operational systems right compounds far more than any single technical decision. He illustrates with concrete practices (procurement workflows, a companywide voting ATS, an internal database called Helix, and continuous peer reviews) that shorten feedback loops and expose problems early.
