## Summary
Travis Kalanick lays out a thesis for 'digitizing the physical world'—treating atoms like bits—and explains how that drives Adams (his post‑Uber company) to build industrial AI for food, mining, and transport. He argues industrial sectors need the CPU/storage/network equivalents (manufacturing, real estate, logistics) plus robotics and software, and that doing so opens multiple multi‑hundred‑billion to trillion‑dollar opportunities. He also stresses the non‑technical challenge: powerful social and political resistance to change that entrepreneurs must overcome.
