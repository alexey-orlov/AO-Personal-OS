## Summary
Alvin Graylin explains why the US–China AI competition is misread as a zero-sum race and should be treated as a stag hunt that requires cooperation to manage systemic risk. He argues Chinese open-weight models and U.S. export controls have reshaped the competitive landscape, that small models already pose real biological/cyber risks, and that Western overinvestment in compute risks creating a fragile financial bubble.
