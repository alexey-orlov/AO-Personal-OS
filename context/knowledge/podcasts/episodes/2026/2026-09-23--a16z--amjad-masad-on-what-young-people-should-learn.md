## Summary
Amjad Masad argues that universities and traditional education are overly rigid and ill-suited to the AI era; instead, schools should enable low-pressure, project-based, curiosity-driven exploration. He believes AI tools and platforms like Replit make highly personalized, hands-on learning practical, and that society should trust and resource young people to experiment rather than funnel them immediately into debt, rigid degrees, or exploitative startup deals. He also proposes practical changes—two-year exploratory tracks, sandboxes for technically curious youth, and using agents/AI to reduce bureaucracy inside companies—so talent can be developed and redeployed productively.
