## Summary
Palmer Luckey argues that autonomous weapons are not a novel Pandora's box but a long-standing class of tools whose modern incarnation simply uses new sensors and compute. He explains Anduril's defense strategy: build specialized, auditable sensor-to-action systems with local autonomy (not centralized LLM-style controllers) because communications, domain constraints, and the need for decisive advantage shape design choices. He is skeptical that a single humanoid general-purpose robot will dominate defense, and stresses practical priorities over apocalyptic AI narratives.
