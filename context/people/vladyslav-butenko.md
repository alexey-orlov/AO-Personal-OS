## What Alex has coached him on

From the [07-24 sync](../areas/softserve/calls/oracle/2026-07-24_133119_one-on-one_vlad-productization-sync.md) — the rules now carried on [packaging-skills](../areas/softserve/packaging-skills.md): name every workflow step against the vendor taxonomy, not only against what we built · absence of a case is a finding, not a blank (no exception handling = an explicit "not covered" row) · differentiate every step across the vertical scenarios, because the differences are the product · prompt the model for candidate differences but adjudicate them yourself ("generation is for coverage, judgment is for truth") · run the vendor-taxonomy gap check as a separate up-front task · finish the table's contents before any formatting · and turn each engagement into self-acceleration skills, named post-factum. His own state on that last one, 2026-07-24: *"in my new projects in Claude it converts easily into skills; I just don't yet keep a skills folder."*

The full record adds the row-by-row half of that coaching (0:35–27:02): rows in workflow order · feature names at the altitude of the task, never the model · the custom-work column means what stays custom *after* the feature is done · merge conceptually identical steps and turn the surviving distinction into a feature · untested is not a capability · a table with no unbuilt rows is evidence it was derived from what we built. Two coaching patterns visible in it, worth carrying forward: **Vlad answers status questions from assumption rather than from the delivered artifact** ("I haven't seen their AltraDocs, I'm assuming"; "that's my hypothesis") — Alex never rejects the assumption, he converts each into a task or a table row; and **he checks that the method landed, not just the fixes** ("Am I managing to convey to you the goal, and the way of moving toward it?") — Vlad's answer was about the go-to-market, i.e. he wanted the *why* first.
