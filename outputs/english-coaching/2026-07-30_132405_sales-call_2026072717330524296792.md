## Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🟡 Non-native construction: *there is no that + noun* — 2 instances

English doesn't allow "no that." Use **no such**, **not that much of a**, or restructure. This one stands out immediately because the word order is impossible in native English.

- "there is **no that** commitment from the customer side" → there's **no such** commitment from the customer side
- "there is **no that potentially adoption, that much of** adoption" → there **isn't that much** adoption potential

**Drill:** When you want to say "no that X," swap to **no such X** or **not that much X**. Rehearse: *no such requirement · not that much overlap · no such guarantee.*

#### 2. 🟡 *commit to* / *adopt* + verb form — 2 instances

*Commit to* takes a **gerund (-ing)**, not a bare infinitive. And keep parallel verbs in the same tense: *have adopted … and are showing*, not "adopted … and showing."

- "they **commit to apply** what we give them" → they **commit to applying** what we give them
- "early adopters who already **adopted and showing**" → early adopters who **have already adopted and are showing**

**Drill:** *commit to* + **-ing** every time — *commit to applying, commit to adopting, commit to shipping.* Mental check: after "to" here, ask "could I say *committed to it*?" If yes → the verb takes *-ing*.

#### 3. 🟡 Redundant / doubled phrasing — 2 instances

Cut words that repeat a meaning already present. *Collaborate* already means "together"; *in place* already means "there."

- "what we **collaborate together**" → what we **build together** / what we **collaborate on**
- "the MCP integration was **not there in place**" → the MCP integration **was not in place**

**Drill:** After a phrase, ask "does the second word add anything?" *collaborate together*, *in place there*, *return back*, *free gift* — drop the passenger word.

#### 4. 🟡 Non-native word order / possessive — 2 instances

Give the recipient with **to**, and prefer the plain possessive.

- "it was just us **demoing them the examples**" → it was just us **demoing the examples to them**
- "we worked on that 3 times with the **customers of ours**" → with **our customers**

**Drill:** *show/demo/give something **to** someone* when the "something" is the focus. And default to *our customers*, not *customers of ours*.

#### 5. 🟡 Overlong, tangled sentences — 2 instances

Two ideas were buried in one 30-word sentence with nested clauses. Break them and lead with the point — a listener on a call can't hold three clauses.

- "the ideal scenario is when at the end of the program, if we go through the first phase, they come out with the group of, let's call them, early adopters who already adopted and showing some demoable…" → **"Ideally, after the first phase they come out with a group of early adopters already showing demoable results with proven ROI across several use cases."**

**Drill:** One sentence = one idea. If you hear yourself add a second "if/when/because" clause, stop and start a new sentence.
