## Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🟡 Missing article before a referent — 3 instances

You drop *the/a* in front of nouns that name a specific or generic thing. In American English these almost always need an article.

- "what **customer** is expecting" → "what **the customer** is expecting"
- "knowledge work that **product manager** does" → "knowledge work that **a product manager** does"
- "sources of truth for **feature matrix**" → "sources of truth for **the feature matrix**"

**Drill:** before any singular countable noun, ask "which one — a specific one (*the*) or one-of-a-kind generic (*a*)?" Almost never zero.

#### 2. 🟡 Wrong preposition in collocation — 4 instances

Fixed verb+preposition pairs are memorized, not derived. These four are the ones that slipped.

- "take a look **on** that file" → "take a look **at** that file"
- "no need **to** customization" → "no need **for** customization"
- "the missing part **on** the SDLC" → "the missing part **of** the SDLC"

**Drill:** lock the pairs — *look **at**, search **for**, no need **for**, part **of***.

#### 3. 🟡 Countability / number — 3 instances

Uncountable nouns don't take plurals or "how many"; compound modifiers stay singular.

- "how **much of intersections**" → "how **much overlap**"
- "This **is** kind of aggregated **artifacts**" → "These **are** aggregated **artifacts**"
- "self-reported **pulse surveys responses**" → "self-reported **pulse-survey responses**"

**Drill:** in a compound noun the first noun is an adjective and stays singular: *pulse-survey response*, *feature list*, *user-research database*.

#### 4. 🟡 False friend (RU/UA influence) — 2 instances

Words that look right but carry a different sense in English.

- "the adoption rate, which is quite **transparent**" → "…which is quite **straightforward**"
- "the **definitive** column" → "the **defining** column"

**Drill:** *transparent* = see-through / honest, not "clear-to-measure". *Definitive* = final/authoritative, not "defining".

#### 5. 🟡 Missing auxiliary in the progressive / "will be" — 2 instances

A progressive needs *is/are*; a future state needs *be*.

- "how **customers looking** to measure that" → "how **the customer is looking** to measure that"
- "they **will much further from truth**" → "they **will be much further from the truth**"

**Drill:** every "-ing" verb needs a *be* in front of it; every "will + adjective" needs *will be*.

#### 6. 🟡 Direct question with no do-support — 1 instance

English forms questions with *do/does*, not by fronting the verb.

- "so **what means handoff** from product to development?" → "so **what does handoff** from product to development **mean**?"

**Drill:** wh-word + **does** + subject + base verb → "what **does** it **mean**?", "how **does** this **work**?"

#### 7. 🟡 Over-long / over-complex sentences — 4 instances

Several points arrive as four-to-five-clause runs that bury the main idea. Break them; one idea per sentence.

- "…given the nature, unstructured nature, and not that traceable nature of knowledge work that product manager does" → "…because a product manager's knowledge work is unstructured and hard to trace"

**Drill:** if you say "nature/thing/part" twice in one sentence, or the verb hasn't arrived after ~15 words, stop and start a new sentence.

#### 8. 🟡 Reversed voice — "be presented to" — 1 instance

You made the menu the thing being presented *to* the client, but the subject is wrong.

- "a menu of skill groups … and **we can be presented to them**" → "…a menu **we can present to them**"

**Drill:** *we present X to them* (active) — only say "X is presented to them" if X, not you, is the subject.
