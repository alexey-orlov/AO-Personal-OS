## Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🟡 Question formed without an auxiliary / inversion — 3 instances

When a yes/no question doesn't start with a wh-word, it needs an auxiliary (*do / does / is / are*) at the front — especially in the "or …?" half of an alternative question. Dropping it makes the question sound like a statement.

- "…or **we also need** Paweł for that?" → …or **do we also need** Paweł for that?
- "…or **Roberta is sufficient** to have everything…?" → …or **is Roberto sufficient** to cover everything…?
- "…or **you have no context**?" → …or **do you have** no context?

**Drill:** before an "or …?" ending, front-load the auxiliary: *"or **do** we…", "or **is** it…", "or **does** he…"*.

#### 2. 🟡 Missing or wrong article — 4 instances

Singular countable nouns almost always need *a/the*. Under speaking pressure the article is the first thing dropped; native listeners notice it immediately.

- "they get **tailored wrapper**" → they get **a tailored wrapper**
- "we ask them to have **mapped feature list**" → …to have **a mapped feature list**
- "show how that helps getting **right results**" → …getting **the right results**
- "whether it's **BA's job**" → whether it's **the BA's job**

**Drill:** every time you say a singular thing (*list, wrapper, job, result*), ask "a or the?" before the noun — never zero.

#### 3. 🟡 Non-native collocation / word choice — 5 instances

Each of these is understood but sounds translated. Swap for the standard native phrase.

- "I would **make a mark** that we have two modes" → I'd **note / flag** that we have two modes
- "I don't want to **go too into details**" → I don't want to **go into too much detail**
- "we will **work in line with Roberta**" → we'll **work with Roberto**
- "I would like to follow up on my **previous speech**" → …follow up on my **earlier point / question**

**Drill:** *note that…* (not "make a mark"), *go into detail* (not "into details"), *work with / alongside someone* (not "in line with").

#### 4. 🟡 Preposition after a verb — 3 instances

The verb chooses the preposition; the wrong one reads as non-native.

- "will **lead into** not that successful experience" → will **lead to** a less successful experience
- "based on your **engagement on** the BA track" → based on your **involvement in** the BA track
- "You will **double-check on** that" → You'll **double-check** that (no preposition)

**Drill:** *lead **to** an outcome*, *involvement **in** something*, *double-check something* (transitive, no *on*).

#### 5. 🟡 Gerund after a preposition — 2 instances

After *commit to* (and *to* as a preposition generally), use the **-ing** form, not the base verb.

- "participants who **commit to apply** whatever we discuss" → who **commit to applying** whatever we discuss
- "they **commit to apply** every session's takeaways" → they **commit to applying** every session's takeaways

**Drill:** *commit to **doing**, look forward to **seeing**, dedicated to **building***. If a verb follows *to*-the-preposition, add *-ing*.

#### 6. 🟡 Answers run long / repetitive — 3 instances

Several key points are grammatical but padded with repetition, so the main idea is harder to hold. Cut duplicated verbs and stacked qualifiers.

- "…not just the skills that we'll **provide to them and tailor to them and show them and make them apply them**…" → …not just the skills we **provide, tailor, and help them apply**…

**Drill:** if you say "…to them" or the same verb three times in one breath, collapse the list: *provide, tailor, and apply* — one object, three verbs.
