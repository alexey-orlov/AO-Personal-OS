## Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🔴 Run-on / overlong sentences — 3 instances

You pack a full situational read into one breath — 3-4 clauses with no full stop — and the listener has to hold too much at once. Land one idea, stop, then start the next.

- "So it means PMs will not be touching in the future any prototypes even though they could be doing that, they will not be doing all those things, so it's still the BA's realm and they are fine with this not that perfect alignment or not." → "So PMs won't touch prototypes going forward, even though they could — that stays the BA's realm. Are they actually fine with that split?"
- "typically it all is, it's always a part of the PM's role… they do user discovery, they do the market research, then they do PRDs, then they do the user stories…" → "Normally this is all the PM's role: discovery, market research, PRDs, user stories, prototyping, feedback triage."

**Drill:** After ~15 words, ask yourself "have I earned a full stop yet?" If the next clause starts a *new* idea, end the sentence first.

#### 2. 🟡 Missing article before institutional nouns — 6+ instances

You drop *the/a* in front of org/role nouns. In American English these almost always need an article.

- "leave it up to **product management unit**" → "leave it up to **the** product management unit"
- "someone from **PDM unit**" → "someone from **the** PDM unit"
- "to get **better understanding**" → "to get **a** better understanding"

**Drill:** Before any singular noun, run the check "*the* (specific one we both know) / *a* (one of several) / nothing (uncountable)". "unit", "understanding", "part", "role" → almost never nothing.

#### 3. 🟡 Adverb placement — 4 instances

Time/frequency adverbs (*now, already, in fact, in the future*) go **before** the main verb or at the clause edge, not wedged between verb and object.

- "We are talking **now** exclusively about product managers" → "We are **now** talking exclusively about product managers"
- "PMs will not be touching **in the future** any prototypes" → "PMs won't be touching any prototypes **in the future**"
- "if we have **already** a product manager" → "if we **already** have a product manager"
- "PMs who are doing **in fact** what their BAs do" → "PMs who are **in fact** doing what their BAs do"

**Drill:** "already / now / in fact" slot in *before* the verb (I already have), never between verb and its object (I have already **it** ✗).

#### 4. 🟡 Question word-order kept as a statement — 3 instances

In embedded questions, keep subject-verb order (no inversion); in *direct* questions, you must invert. You do the opposite.

- "get better understanding of **what's the divide**" → "…of **what the divide is**"
- "decide **what do we start with**" → "decide **what we start with**"
- "or **we have** more constraints?" → "or **do we have** more constraints?"

**Drill:** "*Tell me* where **it is**" (embedded → no inversion) vs. "Where **is it**?" (direct → invert). Embedded question = subject before verb.

#### 5. 🟡 Reflexive *myself* used for *me* — 2 instances

*Myself* is only correct as an echo of an "I" subject in the same clause. As a plain object, use *me*.

- "please include **myself** and Vladislav" → "please include **me** and Vladislav"
- "the PDM… plus **myself**" → "the PDM… plus **me**"

**Drill:** Drop the other person and test: "include *myself*" ✗ → "include *me*" ✓. If "I" isn't the subject of that clause, it's *me*.

#### 6. 🟡 Word order in verb + object + complement — 2 instances

When a verb takes an object plus a "with/there" phrase, the object comes first.

- "let me **get back with that to you** tomorrow" → "let me **get back to you with that** tomorrow"
- "we will need **there the PDM**" → "we will need **the PDM there**"

**Drill:** Fixed frame is "get back **to [person]** with [thing]" — recipient first, content second.

#### 7. 🟡 "as following" → "as follows" — 2 instances

The set phrase is *as follows* (fixed, never "as following"); or just say *like this*.

- "the overall picture looks **as following**" → "the overall picture looks **as follows**"
- "We can do it **as following**" → "We can do it **as follows**" / "like this"

**Drill:** It's frozen: *as follows*, always with the -s, never -ing.

#### 8. 🟡 Present perfect for life experience — 1 instance

"Ever / never (before) in my life" experiences take present perfect, not past simple.

- "but I **never had** this experience before" → "but I've **never had** this experience before"

**Drill:** "up to now, in my life" → *have + past participle* ("I've never done", "have you ever worked").
