### Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🟡 Overlong questions bury or scramble the main clause — 2 instances

Your questions carry the payload in a subordinate clause after 30+ words of setup, and the main verb sometimes collapses. Decide the core question first ("Who owns X — you or someone else?"), say it, then add context.

- "are you still **the right, the key person will be who will be holding** that or you kind of offloading it to someone else?" → are you still **the key person who'll hold** that, or are you offloading it to someone else?
- "…moving that as kind of the sales enablement accelerators, are you still the right… holding that…?" (whole turn ~70 words) → Who owns turning our engagement work into sales-enablement accelerators — you, or someone else?

**Drill:** Before a clarifying question, finish this frame silently: *"Who / what / when + one verb?"* Say that clause first; append context only after.

#### 2. 🟡 Calqued idiom: *push the horses* — 1 instance

*Don't push the horses* is a direct calque of не гони лошадей. English uses a different animal and phrasing.

- "I personally stress, **don't push the horses**." → I'd stress: **let's not rush this** / **hold your horses**.

**Drill:** For "don't rush": *let's not get ahead of ourselves*, *no need to rush*, *hold your horses* (informal).

#### 3. 🟡 Collocation: *consist of both X and Y* — 1 instance

The verb is *consist **of***, and the pairing is *both … **and***, never *both … plus … from*.

- "a joint pod that **consists both from** your people **plus** ours" → a joint pod that **consists of both** your people **and** ours

**Drill:** *consist of* / *be made up of* / *be composed of* — all take *of*. Pair with *both A **and** B*.

#### 4. 🟡 *these kinds of* + article/number agreement — 4 instances

A cluster of small countability/article slips. Two rules cover most: *these/those* forces *kinds* (plural); *a* + a plural noun never co-occur.

- "all **these kind of** … activities" → all **these kinds of** … activities
- "more of **a joint teams**" → more of **a joint team**
- "some kind of **a** joint pod" → some kind of joint pod
- "with **NVIDIA-funded track**" → with **the** NVIDIA-funded track

**Drill:** *these kinds*, *those kinds* (plural agrees). And never say "a … -s": it's *a team* or *teams*, never *a teams*.

#### 5. 🟡 Passive for an embedded team: *embedded in*, not *embedding into* — 1 instance

Teams don't *embed* themselves; they *are embedded*. Use the passive + *in*.

- "FDE teams that **will be embedding into** the customers' projects" → FDE teams that **will be embedded in** the customers' projects

**Drill:** *embedded in* a team / *embedded in* a project (passive, preposition *in*).

#### 6. 🟡 *every next time* → *each time* — 1 instance

*Every next time* is a calque; English uses *each time* / *every time* / *the next time*.

- "**every next time** we will be building something for a new use case" → **each time** we build something for a new use case

**Drill:** *each time*, *every time*, *whenever* — pick one; drop "next".
