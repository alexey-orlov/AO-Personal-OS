## Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🔴🟡 Over-long / over-complex sentences — 4 instances

Your answers are content-rich but frequently run 30–50 words with the core idea buried mid-sentence. Say the point first, then support it. A listener can't hold three stacked clauses.

- "We do have some, but with B2C software-as-a-service products, there is not that much success because consumer cases are not that grounded in … proprietary workflows, and the workflows live within organizations, and that's where you have a lot of hidden, unstructured … knowledge … and being able to package that and infuse those unique perspectives …" → **"Consumer AI-native SaaS rarely wins, because consumer use cases aren't grounded in proprietary workflows. Those workflows live inside organizations — that's the hidden, expert knowledge you can package. That's where B2B has huge room to succeed."**
- "So in the case of that Dev Platform, it's **platform** specifics and specific patterns how people … work and develop with that **platform**, which is special to the **platform** …" → **"For that dev platform, the edge is the platform-specific patterns in how developers actually work — something the model makers won't replicate."**

**Drill:** Before a long answer, decide the one sentence you want them to remember, say it first, then add support. If a sentence passes ~25 words or a third "and", stop and start a new one.

#### 2. 🟡 Wrong preposition in collocation — 5 instances

Several fixed phrases take a specific preposition; a slipped one reads as non-native every time.

- "get a **grasp over** the technology" → "get a **grasp of** the technology"
- "one PM **reporting into** me" → "one PM **reporting to** me"
- "direct that topic **into** a … different direction" → "steer that topic **in** a … direction"
- "**ownership over** the long-term thing" → "**ownership of** the product"

**Drill:** Memorise as chunks — *a grasp **of**, ownership **of**, report **to**, a matter **for** later, head **in** a direction*.

#### 3. 🟡 Subject-verb agreement — 3 instances

Match the verb to the real subject, especially with "there is/are" and singular collective nouns like *model* / *space*.

- "some model capabilities that **is** missing" → "some model capabilities that **are** missing"
- "the decision-making capabilities that the model **have**" → "that the model **has**"
- "there **are** really huge space" → "there **is** a huge space"

**Drill:** Find the noun the verb belongs to and say them back-to-back: *capabilities → are*, *model → has*, *space → is*.

#### 4. 🟡 Non-native verb collocations — 3 instances

The verb chosen isn't the one a native speaker pairs with the noun.

- "it **appeared as** a good chance" → "it **turned out to be** a good opportunity"
- "many startups **go out of the market**" → "many startups **go out of business** / **exit the market**"
- "for models to **grow up** to the point" → "for models to **catch up** to the point"

**Drill:** *grow up* = a child maturing; for capability improving, use *catch up / get there*. For companies dying, *go out of business*.

#### 5. 🟡 Comparison grammar: *comparing to* / *comprises of* — 2 instances

Two textbook slips: the participle after a noun is *compared*, not *comparing*; and *comprise* never takes *of*.

- "the edge you can have **comparing to** the platform players" → "**compared to** the platform players"
- "it always **comprises of** multiple components" → "it always **comprises** multiple components" (or "**is comprised of**")

**Drill:** "X **compared to** Y." And *comprise* = *include* — you'd never say "includes of", so never "comprises of".

#### 6. 🟡 Overloaded multi-clause questions — 2 instances

When a question carries several sub-ideas at once, it collapses. Break it into a lead question plus one qualifier.

- "Maybe last at this stage from my end, curious about the key things they are looking for in the best head of product … Whether it be some experience side or maybe they've distinctive features like whom they are looking for." → **"Maybe one last thing from my end: what are the key things they want in the ideal head of product — specific experience, or particular qualities in the person?"**

**Drill:** One question mark per question. Ask the core thing, pause, then add the "is it more X or Y?" refinement.
