## Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🟡 Article errors with nouns and set phrases — 5 instances

Articles are the biggest single leak in this transcript. Three sub-rules cover all five: (a) *another* already contains "an" — never say "the another"; (b) the fixed phrase is **the same**, never "that same"; (c) generic plurals ("customers" in general) take no article, while a unique role ("the product lead") takes *the*.

- "And **the another** case when it's not sufficient" → And **the other** case is when it's not sufficient
- "hopefully we will do **that same** with AI Lakehouse" → hopefully we'll do **the same** with AI Lakehouse
- "every solution we envision to be implemented for **the customers**" → every solution we build for **customers**

**Drill:** *the other* / *another* — never both. *do the same*, *say the same*, *think the same*. Generic plural → no article: "for customers", "for enterprises", "for partners". Unique role → *the*: "I'm **the** product lead for this relationship."

#### 2. 🟡 "bring up" used for "walk us through" — 2 instances

*Bring up* means to raise or mention a topic for the first time. It does not mean "present in detail" or "give me an example of". When you're asking someone to expand on something, use **walk us through**, **give us**, or **take us through**.

- "Maybe I can ask you to **bring up** some customer scenarios" → Maybe I can ask you to **walk us through** some customer scenarios
- "if probably you can **bring up** some particular use case" → if you could **give us** a specific use case

**Drill:** *bring up* = introduce a topic ("he brought up pricing"). *Walk through / take through / give* = expand on it. When you're asking for detail, never *bring up*.

#### 3. 🟡 Adverb placement before the verb cluster — 2 instances

RU/UA lets adverbs float; English fixes them. *Literally*, *just*, *probably*, *maybe* go **after** the modal and **before** the main verb — not between subject and modal.

- "the customer **literally can use just** the Lakehouse" → the customer **can literally just use** the Lakehouse
- "if **probably you can** bring up some particular use case" → if you could **maybe** bring up a specific use case

**Drill:** the slot is *subject → modal → adverb → verb*: "we **can just** do it", "they **will probably** need it", "I **can literally** see it." Say the modal first, then the adverb.

#### 4. 🔴 "more easy" → "easier" — 1 instance

One-and two-syllable adjectives take **-er**, never *more*. This is a textbook-level error that stands out sharply against otherwise fluent speech.

- "it will be **more easy** to comprehend" → it'll be **easier** to follow

**Drill:** short adjective → *-er*: easier, simpler, clearer, faster, harder. *More* + adjective only for long ones: *more complicated*, *more efficient*, *more relevant*. Rule of thumb: if it's ≤2 syllables, *-er*.

#### 5. 🔴 Multi-clause self-description collapsed mid-sentence — 1 instance

The role description ran three grammatical patterns into each other and broke: *envision* doesn't take "to be implemented", and "as the not a bespoke solution" strands two articles against each other. Say the role in one clean clause.

- "my role is to think about **every solution we envision to be implemented for the customers as the not a bespoke solution but rather the package thing, repeatable offer**" → my role is to make sure **every solution we build for customers ships as a packaged, repeatable offer rather than a bespoke one**

**Drill:** *envision* takes a noun or -ing form: "we envision **a platform**", "we envision **building** it" — never "we envision **to be implemented**". And keep the contrast simple: **"as X, not Y"** or **"not as X but as Y"** — pick one frame, don't blend them.

#### 6. 🟡 Conditional: "if you can … it will be" → "if you could … it would be" — 1 instance

When you're making a polite request rather than stating a fact, both halves shift back: *if you could* … *it would be*. Keeping "can/will" makes the request sound like a demand or a prediction.

- "if probably you **can** bring up some particular use case, it **will** be more easy" → if you **could** bring up a specific use case, it **would** be easier

**Drill:** polite-request pattern: *If you could X, it would be helpful / that would be great / it'd be easier to follow.* Two steps back, both halves.
