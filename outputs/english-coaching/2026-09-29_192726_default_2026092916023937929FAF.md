### Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🔴 Run-on sentence that buries the point — 5 instances

When you propose something, say it first in one short sentence, then add the reasons. Every extra clause and hedge pushes the point further back. Your key idea for Oscar arrived in the fourth clause of an 85-word sentence, and the next speaker restated the AIDP goal as if it had not been made (inferred).

- "But as far as I understand, given the situation where we are right now with AIDP and alike, … probably the reverse mode should be more appropriate, and we were about to actually discuss that because probably we might want to have **some packs prepared upfront** for some typical setups … so that we have those demos before we show up with customers." → For AIDP, I think the reverse makes more sense: **prepare packs upfront** for typical setups, so we have demos ready before we meet customers.
- "So there are **different buckets of those product categories where we are going to have the AI packs built by SoftServe folding into in different topics**: …" → We group the AI packs SoftServe builds **into topic buckets**: …
- "My name is Alex, and I'm with Bogdan, leading specifically the productization efforts that we do on our end … **, based in the US, though now traveling in Europe**." → I'm Alex. I work with Bogdan and lead productization. **I'm based in the US**, traveling in Europe right now.

**Drill:** Headline first: the proposal in under 15 words, then "because…". One idea per sentence, one hedge at most. If you hear yourself pass a third comma, stop and start a new sentence.

#### 2. 🟡 Set phrase mangled or cut short — 2 instances

Fixed phrases work only in their exact form. *And the like* needs *the*. *In the process* needs *of doing something* after it; otherwise say *in progress*.

- "right now with AIDP **and alike**" → right now with AIDP **and the like**
- "on our end, and **we are in the process**" → on our end, and **it's still in progress**

**Drill:** *AIDP and the like* (= and similar things). *Alike* only closes a pair: *customers and partners alike*. *We're in the process of building it* · *It's still in progress* · *It's a work in progress*.

#### 3. 🟡 *want* + object + *to*-infinitive — 1 instance

When you ask whether someone wants *you* to do something, English needs the object: *want me to*. Without *me*, "do you want to show…" asks whether *they* want to present, which is not what you meant.

- "do you **want to show** how that is structured visually" → do you **want me to show** how that is structured visually

**Drill:** *хотите, чтобы я…* = *do you want me to…*: *Do you want me to share my screen?* · *Would you like me to walk you through it?* · *They expect us to deliver.*

#### 4. 🟡 One determiner per noun phrase — 1 instance

*Some* and *certain* both mark the noun as unspecified, so English takes one of them, not both (a calque of *какие-то определённые*). After *certain*, a countable noun usually goes plural.

- "**some certain category** of tasks" → **certain categories** of tasks

**Drill:** *certain categories* · *some categories* · *a certain category*. Never two determiners in a row: not *some certain*, *this our*, *the some*.

#### 5. 🟡 RU calque: time expression — 1 instance

*In some time* is a word-for-word *через какое-то время*; natives say *at some point*, *in a while*, *soon* or *later*. The phrase comes up whenever you promise a follow-up, so it is worth fixing now.

- "we'll share that with you **maybe in some time**" → we'll share that with you **at some point**

**Drill:** *at some point* (vague) · *in a couple of weeks* (committed) · *shortly* (days). *In* + time needs a number: *in two weeks*, *in a month*.
