### Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🟡 Run-on sentence that buries the point — 5 instances

When you ask or propose something, say it first in one short sentence, then add the context. Your three main asks were clear enough that Dmytro and Karsten answered them, but each took 40–85 words to arrive, inside chains of *and*, *whatever* and *because* clauses.

- "**I mean, I will probably get back again and figure out maybe for some political reasons it doesn't work this way**, but I'll reiterate again." → **Maybe this won't work for political reasons**, but let me say it again.
- "curious if we have any, at least bits of insights into **what types of NATO-related projects this Oracle team is has visibility into** and what their scope … and so on and so forth because there might be multiple scenarios." → Do we know **which NATO projects this Oracle team can see**, and what their scope is? I see two scenarios.
- "Maybe we can have some kind of a conversation with them, which is more of a discovery conversation to learn **whatever they know and whatever they feel is good for NATO, whatever is relevant to their specific counterparts**, because they don't work with all the countries, right?" → Let's have a discovery conversation with them: **what do they know, what do they think NATO needs, and who exactly do they work with there?**

**Drill:** Question first, under 15 words, ending on a question mark (*Can we flip the approach?*), then the context, one idea per sentence. When you hear your third *and*, *whatever* or *because*, stop and start a new sentence.

#### 2. 🟡 Wrong preposition in collocation — 5 instances

You have a question **for** someone (*a question to you* is a calque of *вопрос к вам*), and a hint is **about** something. *Into* belongs to *insight into* and *visibility into*, and you used both correctly in the same turn. *Question to you* opened three of your questions, which makes it the most repeated non-native marker in the call.

- "I have a follow-up **question to** you, Dmitry" → I have a follow-up **question for** you, Dmitry
- "**Question to** you, Mitro" → **Question for** you, Mitro
- "Do they have any **hints into** what this infrastructure is used for?" → Do they have any **hints about** what this infrastructure is used for?

**Drill:** *a question for you* · *a hint about it* · *insight into* · *visibility into* · *interest in*. *To* only in *put a question to someone* and *my question to you is…*

#### 3. 🟡 Conditionals: no *will/would* after *if* — 2 instances

After *if*, English uses a plain tense: present for a real plan, past for a hypothetical. *Will* or *would* goes only in the other half. *If he will give* and *if I'd be coming* carry the Russian/Ukrainian future and *бы* straight across.

- "And **if he will give** us a list and **confirm**" → And **if he gives** us a list and **confirms**
- "because **if I'd be coming** to you knowing nothing about you … **It's going to be** weird, right?" → because **if I came** to you knowing nothing about you … **it would be** weird, right?

**Drill:** *If he gives us a list, we'll draft offers.* · *If I came to you cold, it would be weird.* Keep *will* and *would* out of the *if* half.

#### 4. 🟡 Wrong connector: *as long as* for *since* — 1 instance

*As long as* sets a condition ("provided that"), so the listener hears a condition where you meant a reason. To give a reason, say *since* or *given that*.

- "**as long as** they were not **that much interested**, as Dmitry said" → **since** they were not **that interested**, as Dmitry said

**Drill:** *since they weren't interested* (reason) · *as long as they're interested* (condition) · *given that* (formal reason). If *provided that* fits, keep *as long as*; if not, say *since*.

#### 5. 🟡 One determiner per noun phrase — 1 instance

*Some* and a possessive like *Oracle's* both fill the slot before the noun, so English takes one of them: *some Oracle products* (Oracle as a label) or *some of Oracle's products*.

- "NATO is using **some Oracle's products**" → NATO is using **some Oracle products**

**Drill:** *some Oracle products* · *some of Oracle's products* · *Oracle's products*. One determiner before a noun: not *some Oracle's*, *the some*, *this our*.

#### 6. 🟡 *as* + adjective + *as possible* — 1 instance

The pattern is *as + adjective + as possible*, with no *much* and no *it is*; *as much open as it is possible* carries *настолько открытый, насколько это возможно* over word for word. *As much as possible* works only without an adjective: *talk to them as much as possible*.

- "**as much open as it is possible**" → **as open as possible**

**Drill:** *as open as possible* · *as soon as possible* · *as simple as possible* · *as much as possible* (no adjective). Nothing goes between the second *as* and *possible*.
