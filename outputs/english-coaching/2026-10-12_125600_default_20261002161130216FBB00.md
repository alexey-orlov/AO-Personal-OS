#### 2. 🟡 Set phrase mangled: *get up to speed* — 1 instance

The idiom for learning something well enough to contribute is *get up to speed* (on something); *get to the pace* is not an English phrase. In the next sentence you reached for it again with *speed up* and *be on track* (inferred), so it is worth fixing now.

- "for people who will be working with that **to get to the pace** as fast as possible" → for people who will be working with that **to get up to speed** as fast as possible

**Drill:** *get up to speed on AIDP* · *bring our engineers up to speed* · *ramp up* (verb). *Speed up* = go faster; *on track* = on schedule; *up to speed* = trained enough to contribute.

#### 3. 🟡 Non-native verb-noun collocation — 1 instance

When English has a verb for the action, use it instead of *do the* + noun + *of*. The noun version adds words and sounds translated.

- "curious if you also **do the customizations of**, uh, **and setups of** the data pipelines" → curious if you also **customize and set up** the data pipelines

**Drill:** *customize X* · *set up X* · *configure X* · *migrate X*. When you hear *do the …-ation of*, swap in the verb.
