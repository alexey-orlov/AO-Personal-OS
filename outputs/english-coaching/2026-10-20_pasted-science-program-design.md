### Top items to study this session

#### 1. 🟡 Run-on / overlong sentence — 10 instances

Spoken English carries one point per sentence. When you stack three or four clauses (conditions, asides, "and also be ready to…") before the main verb, the listener loses the point, and so do you: this is where the double negative (item 8) and most of the §2 awkwardness came from.

- "3, I think we need to mold into the program **that this is a constraint that extended discovery is something that** we definitely will have within this engagement…" → **Third: extended discovery has to be built into the program.** It will take longer than in other Jumpstarts…
- "If they are really reluctant to expose stuff and they are really in observer mode, which might be the case, while still they have an ambition and funding to, to kind of pursue and participate in this program, **It could be a training program**…" → If they're reluctant to share data and stay in observer mode, but still have the funding to join, **it could be a lighter training program.**
- "probably we should deliberately add, let's say, **a month at least. I don't know, at least a month** after we are done with all the trainings…" → the transformation version should add **at least a month** after the training…

**Drill:** Number, point, full stop: "Third: X. Here's why: Y." When a sentence passes ~20 words, end it and start a new one. Asides go after the point, never before it.

#### 2. 🟡 Verb pattern copied from RU/UA (preposition / *to* / *-ing* after the verb) — 6 instances

Many English verbs fix what comes after them, and the Russian/Ukrainian pattern doesn't carry over: *объяснить нам* is *explain **to** us*, *потратить неделю, чтобы…* is *spend a week **using***.

- "Can we **elaborate this** a little bit" → Can we **elaborate on this** a little bit
- "**explain us something**" → **explain something to us**
- "they **spend** the **upcoming** week **to use** that" → they **spend** the **following** week **using** that

**Drill:** *elaborate **on** X* · *explain X **to** Y* · *ground X **in** Y* · *spend time **doing*** · *get someone **to** do* / *have someone do* · *promise **(that) we'll**…*

#### 3. 🟡 *will / would* after *if / when* — 4 instances

After *if / when / once / after*, English uses the present for future meaning; *will* goes only in the result half. *If you'll have time* is a direct copy of *если у тебя будет время*.

- "if you**'ll have** time to review" → if you **have** time to review
- "when we **will be merging** everything" → when we **merge** everything
- "if you**'d ask** me" → if you **ask** me

**Drill:** "If you **have** time, you**'ll** get Thursday too." Present after *if*, *will* after the comma. Listen for *'ll* or *'d* right after *if/when*; if it's there, cut it.

#### 4. 🟡 Near-miss verb (right idea, wrong word) — 4 instances

The verb is in the right neighborhood but means something else in English: *unveil* is for launches, *infuse* is for flavor or energy, *mold* is for shaping material.

- "observe how they change the work, **unveil** some hidden stuff" → …, **uncover** some hidden stuff
- "make sure that I **infuse** everything **fresh** that was shared" → make sure I **incorporate** everything **new** that was shared
- "we need to **mold into** the program that…" → we need to **build into** the program that…

**Drill:** *uncover* problems / *unveil* a product · *incorporate* feedback / *infuse* energy · *build (bake) X into* a plan · *say it out loud* / *speak* a language.

#### 5. 🟡 Set phrase / idiom bent — 5 instances

Idioms are frozen: change the article or one word and they sound non-native or change meaning. *Double down on* means committing harder to a bet you already made, not worrying more about a risk.

- "create something **in the vacuum**" → create something **in a vacuum**
- "they are just a **blank slate page**" → they are just a **blank slate**
- "I'm **doubling down on this situation** that might happen" → I'm **even more convinced this** might happen

**Drill:** *in a vacuum* · *a blank slate* (or *a blank page*, not both) · *I'm pretty sure* · *let's see where it takes us* · *double down on* + a strategy you already chose.

#### 6. 🟡 Adverb placement (*probably*, *still*, *also*) — 5 instances

*Probably* and *still* go between the subject and the main verb (after *should/will*). Sentence-first *Probably…* copies *Наверное, …*, and sentence-final *also* copies *тоже*.

- "**probably we should** deliberately add" → **we should probably** deliberately add
- "**Probably we need** to touch upon the topic of team composition" → **We probably need** to touch upon the topic of team composition
- "while **still they** have an ambition and funding" → while **they still** have an ambition and funding

**Drill:** Subject + *probably* + verb: *We should probably…*, *We probably need…*. To open with a hedge, say *Maybe we should…*. At the end of a sentence use *as well* or *too*, never *also*.

#### 7. 🟢 Articles (*a / the*) — 8 instances

*The customer* is the specific client everyone is discussing, so it always takes *the*; a first mention of one thing takes *a/an*. Single article drops can be ASR, but *customer* without *the* repeats three times.

- "whatever **customer** picks" → whatever **the customer** picks
- "They had **very specific question**" → They had **a very specific question**
- "we kind of create **the** acceleration award" → we kind of create **an** acceleration award

**Drill:** Say *the customer* as one word: *the-customer picks*, *the-customer is happy*. New thing → *a/an*; a thing everyone already knows → *the*; generic plural → no article.

#### 8. 🔴 Double negative after *I don't think* — 1 instance

*I don't think* already carries the negation, so the verb after it stays positive. You opened with *I don't think*, inserted a 12-word aside, then restarted with *we cannot*, so the sentence literally says we *can* expect them to transform their processes, the opposite of your point.

- "**I don't think**, given our current understanding of the space and readiness to promise something, **we cannot** expect them to transform the processes after those sessions" → **I don't think**, given our current understanding of the space…, **we can** expect them to transform the processes after those sessions

**Drill:** One negation per sentence. If an aside has pushed the verb far from *I don't think*, drop the frame and restart cleanly: "**We can't** expect them to transform their processes right after the sessions."
